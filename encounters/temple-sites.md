# Temple Sites — sacred-site visits

Stubs for temple visits in the Avatar mold: the party sails to a sacred
site, meets the tradition that keeps it, and leaves having learned
something about how Nila works. Each site is a real-world mythology run
straight — one cosmology per region (see
[campaign overview §4, §5](../lore/campaign-overview.md)).

Each does three jobs: it connects to a specific PC, it shows a piece of
the world's mechanics, and it sets the players inside a strange
primary-source myth.

Legend: `[draft]` = invented here, to keep, cut, or rename.

---

## Kali's garland — Kalikhat `[draft]`

Real myth: Kali wears a necklace of fifty severed heads, one for each
letter of the Sanskrit alphabet.

The idol stands over a floor of old bone. Fifty heads hang at its neck,
and the priests can tell you which sound each one carries. They hold that
every name ever spoken is strung there, and that to know a thing's name
is to hold its head.

- **Reveals:** the name quest, said out loud by people who live it — a
  name is a thing you can own and carry. The party gathers names for Preem
  with no word for what they are doing; here a temple has the word.
- **Lands on:** whichever PC has begun asking what a name does to the
  thing it belongs to.
- **Region fit:** Kalikhat — the South Asian region, and the country the
  Underdark peoples fled into after the Bleeding Star.

## Mímir's well — Musleheim `[draft]`

Real myth: Odin gave an eye to drink from the well of wisdom under the
world-tree. The god Mímir was beheaded; Odin kept the head, and it still
speaks and gives counsel.

A spring in a cave, a head resting at the bottom of the clear water,
awake. It answers any question truthfully. It names the price first — an
eye, a memory, a year off the end of a life — and will not speak until
the price is paid.

- **Reveals:** the bargain the Order of Sunne makes, before the party
  knows what a warforged is — knowledge bought with pieces of the self.
- **Lands on:** Aniess, who can ask it what she was before the Order
  remade her. It will tell her, for a price.
- **Region fit:** Musleheim — the Norse fire-island.

## The chant of descent — the Kurunatukas `[draft]`

Real practice: the Hawaiian genealogical chant (the Kumulipo) recites
creation from the first living thing forward to the person present.

The priests sit you down and sing your ancestry backward, generation by
generation. Carried to the end, the line arrives at the first dragon that
fell from the sky and became an island. A person whose blood the chant
cannot carry back to a dragon, they set aside — no place in the song.

- **Reveals:** the comet cycle, said aloud for the first time — dragons
  fall, live an age, become islands. The players hear the shape of it with
  no lecture.
- **Lands on:** Sam, whose dragonborn line sits closest to that first
  dragon.
- **Region fit:** the Kurunatukas — the Polynesian islands.
