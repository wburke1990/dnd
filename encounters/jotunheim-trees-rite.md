## What is wrong at the trees

Two dead of the forest-nameless of northwest Musleheim lay down and became an ash and
an elm. On the march to retake Lonka ~1,900 years ago the zealots took them as
cuttings, carried them under the sea, and rooted them at the high point of the
ring-wall. They named them Askr and Embla and taught them they are the children of the
primordial dragon. Cut off from the forest's common stock, the two had only the
zealots' voices, and they hold that account as their own memory. Asked, they give it
back; the zealots teach it to each new generation at the trees. Pax is the one
forest-nameless still joined to the common stock, and carries the memory these two
lost.

So there are three things to undo: a name laid on them, a past they were taught, and a
root cut off from the stock the memory comes from.

---

## What the rite is, and what it is not

A church where blood was spilled or a body left is **violated**, and cannot be used
until a bishop **reconciles** it — water, salt, ashes and wine, sprinkled, with the
psalms. The first consecration is *not* repeated. Reconciliation restores a place to
what it already was; it does not make the place the bishop's.

That is what Pax performs. He is not consecrating the trees, not claiming them, and
not naming them. He restores two nameless dead to the stock they were taken from. Say
this at the table in those words. The alternative — a second man arriving to name them
and take them — is what the zealots did, and Pax could do the same thing.

---

## The book

The zealots keep the rite in writing, in the manner of the period: one book holding
the dedication of holy ground, the reconciliation of ground that has been violated,
the renunciations, and the ordeals. Their own consecration of the two trees is in it,
and the rite for undoing a false one is three leaves further on, because a book of
that kind carries both.

The party takes it, or copies it, or is shown it. `[OPEN]` where — the zealot temple in
the caldera, a captured priest, or the drowned ruins.

Real-world model: the **Pontificale Romano-Germanicum**, compiled at St Alban's,
Mainz, c. 950–962, and the working liturgical book of Latin Europe for the next two
centuries. The book at the table is a pontifical.

## The rite — the seven stages

Each stage is a real rite of the period. The Latin is meant to be read aloud at the
table.

### 1. The vigil outside — *pernoctatio*

In the dedication rite the relics wait outside the walls overnight and enter in the
morning. Pax brings what the trees were cut off from: leaf-litter, root, rot and
seed out of the forests of northwest Musleheim, carried across the sea in the
Compost Staff or in cloth. He keeps the night with it at the foot of the ring-wall,
outside the zealots' ground.

He brings nothing else onto the ground.

### 2. The knocking — *Tollite portas*

At the dedication the bishop strikes the shut door three times with his staff and
calls, and a deacon hidden inside answers, and only on the third exchange does the
door open. Psalm 23 (Vulgate):

> *Tollite portas, principes, vestras, et elevamini, portae aeternales: et introibit
> Rex gloriae.*
> — *Quis est iste Rex gloriae?*

Pax strikes each trunk three times with the staff. The trees answer in their own
voices with the past the zealots taught them, as they always do when asked. They give
it three times; he answers each time with the memory he carries.

`[mechanic — proposed]` There is no single check that ends this. Each round he must give a
detail of the forest-dead the zealots could not have supplied and the trees have no
way to have invented — how their people's dead lie down, what the litter smells of,
what the elm's roots gave him. Ask Andrew to speak them. Details he cannot supply stay
as the trees have them.

### 3. The renunciation — *abrenuntiatio*

Nothing is taken off anyone without their consent. The relic-thefts of the 9th–11th
centuries were defended on exactly that ground: the saint would not have permitted the
removal unless she wished to go. So the trees must renounce the name themselves,
aloud, in their own voices, and Pax cannot say it for them.

The baptismal form, and the Old Saxon vow that names the gods being put down:

> *Abrenuntias?* — *Abrenuntio.*
>
> *forsachistu diabolae? — ec forsacho diabolae. end allum dioboles wercum? — ec
> forsacho allum dioboles wercum and wordum, Thunaer ende Woden ende Saxnote ende
> allum them unholdum the hira genotas sint.*

For the trees, the thing renounced is the name and the story:

> *Abrenuntias nomini tibi imposito?*
> *Abrenuntias patri quem non habuisti?*

`[OPEN]` whether one renounces and one refuses. The ash and the elm have stood there
1,900 years being told the same thing. The two can answer differently.

### 4. The scraping — *rasura*

When a priest was degraded, the chrism was scraped off his anointed thumbs and
fingers with a blade or a shard of glass. The record of his ordination was struck and the
chrism was taken off his hands. (The scraping is in the pontificals from the 12th
century on.)

Pax scrapes the bark where the zealots anointed them and where the two names are cut
in. The names come off in shavings, which are kept — they are needed at stage 5.

It takes time, uses his hands, and can be interrupted. If the zealots are
going to arrive, they arrive here.

### 5. The restitution — *restitutio*

The rule the medieval canonists took from Augustine and Gratian put in the *Decretum*
(c. 1140): the sin is not forgiven until the thing taken is given back.

> *Non remittitur peccatum, nisi restituatur ablatum.*

The trees cannot be carried home. So the material is brought to them: the litter and stock
Pax carried are worked into the ground at the roots, with the shavings, and the roots
reach the common stock again. What the two lost, the stock still holds, and it is
handed back to them the way it is handed to any of the forest-dead.

> *Reddatur silvae quod silvae ablatum est.*

Here the trees get their past out of the stock, not out of Pax's account of it. The
stock is where the nameless have always got their memory.

### 6. The blood

A great tree comes into power and wisdom by blood. That rule holds here. Three kinds
of blood are available:

- **Other people's, spilled.** What the zealots used — the Maalm colonists, killed on
  this ground, and they called it reconsecration. This is the thing that made the
  place violated in the first place.
- **A martyr's, deposited.** No altar is an altar without the bones of someone who
  died for it sealed underneath.
- **The celebrant's own, offered.** Odin gave an eye at the well under the tree and
  took up the runes; the well is already in the world
  ([temple sites](temple-sites.md)).

Pax opens his own arm at the roots. It costs him, and it does not make the trees his.

`[for Doug]` **Consecration is not grace.** The rite works; it compels nobody. Blood
consecrates ground and feeds trees, and it does not buy the favour of a god — which is
why the zealots' plan at Moku fails no matter how many they kill
([Lonka / Jotunheim](../lore/lonka-jotunheim.md) §6). The psalm sung while a violated
church is reconciled says it outright: *"The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a
broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise"* (Psalm 51:17, KJV — the
*Miserere*, whose seventh verse is the *Asperges*, "Purge me with hyssop, and I shall
be clean"). Same in Hosea 6:6 and 1 Samuel 15:22.

`[OPEN]` the price, and whether the trees ask for it or he offers first. The
[Mímir's well](temple-sites.md) model — the price is named first and the thing will
not speak until it is paid — applies here.

### 7. The name struck, and no other laid on `[settled — Will]`

**The trees end nameless.** A name could be struck from the list read at the altar,
and nothing put in its place.

> *Deleatur nomen, nec aliud imponatur.*

Askr and Embla are struck. The two are nameless again, which in this world is what
they were and what their people are: part of the common stock, no one's. They go back
into the land and are not held by anyone. Pax gives them no name.

If he names them, he has done what the zealots did, and the rite has only moved them
from the zealots to him. Andrew should be allowed to do it at the table — the ending
is settled as what the rite is for, not as what he is made to choose.

`[proposed]` **This is also what stops the second planting.** The zealots mean to sail
to Moku and do at that island what they did here, and cuttings off these two would go
with the fleet ([Lonka / Jotunheim](../lore/lonka-jotunheim.md) §6). Two trees with no
names are two trees there is nothing to carry.

## What it costs Pax

He was named by something in the stars and cannot remember the name
([Pax — DM notes](../characters/pax-dm-notes.md)). The rite turns on names being laid
on and taken off, and his own name is in the same condition.

`[proposed]` To strike a name he has to use his own. Whether he recovers it here, and
whether he keeps it afterward, is the price. The standing rule holds either way: the
rite works or fails on the memory he carries out of the forest, which is real, so
nothing that happens here settles whether the Green Revelation is real, whether he was
chosen, or whether he is deluded.

At [the hell-tree](the-hell-tree.md) he stands in front of a consecration that is
working — a tree kept in power by the deaths around it, and a cult that runs the
killing without going near the tree. He sees the rite performed before he performs the
undoing. `[OPEN]` how much of the mechanism the party can read there, and

## Sources

Pontificale Romano-Germanicum (Mainz, c. 950–962) — the dedication of a church, the
reconciliation of a violated church, the renunciations. Psalm 23 (Vulg.), *Tollite
portas*. The Old Saxon Baptismal Vow (c. 800). Gratian, *Decretum* (c. 1140), on
restitution, quoting Augustine, *Ep.* 153. The rite of degradation and the scraping of
the anointed fingers (12th–13th c. pontificals). Guibert of Nogent, *De pignoribus
sanctorum* (c. 1119–25) — a false relic's cult is worth nothing however sincere its
devotion. Patrick Geary, *Furta Sacra*, for the consent doctrine behind the
9th–11th century relic thefts. Adam of Bremen, *Gesta* IV (c. 1075) — the grove at
Uppsala, where the trees are held divine from the blood of what is hung in them.
