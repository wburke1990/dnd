# The Game Preserve

## A D&D Encounter — Design Note `[draft]`

The setting around [the hell-tree](the-hell-tree.md): a hunting ground in the
deadly wild land at the northern tip of [Suartleheim](../lore/nila-atlas.md). An
**independent operator — not the [Company](../lore/the-company.md)** — runs it for
profit: clients pay to hunt the game, and to hunt people. The
[hell-tree](the-hell-tree.md) stands at its center, holy and held by no one. The
operator works the outer land but does not own the tree, and the Company cannot buy
it — which is why [Preem](../characters/preem.md) needs the party, to get in through
the hunt and reach a center no one controls.

Real-world sources: *The Most Dangerous Game* (Zaroff hunts the men who wash up on
his land) for the hunt, and *Heart of Darkness* (a journey inward toward a ruined
man at the center) for the approach.

---

## Where it is

The northern tip of Suartleheim — **not an island**, the land's end north of
[Raand](../lore/nila-atlas.md) (94, 74), across the strait from
[Sukkek](../lore/nila-atlas.md) (95, 61). The land itself kills — the poison of
[Anchar](../handouts/anchar.md) near the tree, the wild country around it — which is
what makes it huntable ground: the danger is already here. The beasts are wild
beasts, not demons.

---

## Getting in

Three ways, and the fee sets which one the party takes.

- **Pay to hunt.** A client buys entry as a hunter — **5,000 gold.** The party will
  not have it.
- **Sign up to be hunted.** The way in the party can afford, short of sneaking: put
  your name down as the quarry, and the paying hunters come after you.
- **Sneak in** past the fence and the deadly land.

---

## The approach — a Heart of Darkness escalation

The way to the tree runs inward, and it worsens with each stretch: the poison
thickening, the land and its game turning stranger, the pull of the
[thing in the tree](the-hell-tree.md) growing the closer they come. The tree, and
the ruined mind held in it, is the man at the end of the river. `[OPEN]` the stages
of the escalation.
