# Spells as Poems

A brainstorming list for reflavoring spells as recited poems, the way the
family's *Dream of the Blue Veil* is cast by reciting
[*Wynken, Blynken, and Nod*](../handouts/wynken-blynken-and-nod.md) (played
in [Session 8](../sessions/session-08.md), where the party read it verse by
verse with Ali). This is a DM working doc, not a player handout — when we
settle on one, the poem itself gets saved verbatim into `handouts/` with its
real-world attribution.

## How it works

- **The poem is the incantation.** The party casts by reciting it.
- **The images are the effect.** Pick a poem whose pictures are what the
  spell does. *Wynken* is a boat on a river with stars above and below —
  which is the dream the spell drops them into. The chill landed because the
  caster didn't know the lullaby described a real river.
- **Foreshadow without the caster knowing.** The strongest pairings describe
  what waits before the party has seen it.
- **Verse count maps to casters.** *Wynken* is four verses; four people read
  one each. Match the split to the poem's stanzas.

## Candidates

Grouped by school. Each is a spell, the poem, the poet and dates, and what
the poem's images match. `[now]` marks ones that fit the current campaign —
the sea voyage out of Maalm and the Egyptian death-road.

### Evocation

- **Call Lightning / Control Weather** — "The Cloud" (Percy Bysshe Shelley,
  1792–1822). The cloud speaks as itself, carrying hail and thunder and then
  dissolving in rain — the weather effects stated line by line. `[now]` fits
  the pirate/patrol attack planned for the next voyage.
- **Gust of Wind / Control Winds / Wind Wall** — "Ode to the West Wind"
  (Shelley). An address to the wind as a driving force.
- **Fireball / mass fire** — "The Destruction of Sennacherib" (Lord Byron,
  1788–1824). An army consumed in a single night.
- **Darkness** — "Darkness" (Byron). The sun extinguished and the world left
  lightless.

### Conjuration

- **Magnificent Mansion / Hallucinatory Terrain / Demiplane** — "Kubla Khan"
  (Samuel Taylor Coleridge, 1772–1834). A pleasure-dome decreed into being
  over a sacred river and caverns.
- **Summon from the deep (an aberration, a kraken)** — "The Kraken" (Alfred,
  Lord Tennyson, 1809–1892). A thing that sleeps in the abyss until it rises.
  `[now]` rhymes with the deep under Maalm and Apep on the dream river.
- **Pass Without Trace / a hidden path** — "The Way Through the Woods"
  (Rudyard Kipling, 1865–1936). A road erased and hidden, walkable only by
  those who know it was there.

### Divination

- **Speak with Dead** — "Because I could not stop for Death" (Emily
  Dickinson, 1830–1886). The carriage ride with Death; the tone of the Duat.
  `[now]` the party is escorting a soul through the underworld.
- **Augury** — "The Raven" (Edgar Allan Poe, 1809–1849). A question put to
  the dead and one word returned.
- **Foresight / a portent** — "The Second Coming" (W. B. Yeats, 1865–1939). A
  revelation at hand and a shape moving to be born.

### Enchantment

- **Charm / Compulsion with a cost** — "La Belle Dame sans Merci" (John
  Keats, 1795–1821). A knight enthralled to his ruin. An NPC's spell, not a
  PC's.
- **Sleep / Hypnotic Pattern / a lull** — "The Lotos-Eaters" (Tennyson). A
  land where it is always afternoon and the will to move drains away.
- **Command / Heroism** — "Invictus" (William Ernest Henley, 1849–1903).
  Mastery asserted — over a target, or over the caster's own fear.

### Necromancy

- **Bestow Curse / Hex / Brew Potion** — the witches' chant "Double, double
  toil and trouble" (Shakespeare, *Macbeth*, c. 1606). The cauldron rhyme;
  fits a hag.
- **Blight / Contagion** — "The Sick Rose" (William Blake, 1757–1827). An
  invisible worm destroying a life from within.
- **Revivify / Raise Dead** — "Do not go gentle into that good night" (Dylan
  Thomas, 1914–1953). A refusal of death.

### Transmutation

- **Fly / Levitate** — "High Flight" (John Gillespie Magee Jr., 1922–1941).
  Earth's bonds slipped, the sky flown on wings.
- **Water Breathing / a sea-change** — "Full fathom five" (Ariel's song,
  Shakespeare, *The Tempest*, c. 1610). A body changed by the sea into
  "something rich and strange." `[now]` fits the coming sea voyage.
- **Polymorph / a fae shapeshift** — "The Song of Wandering Aengus" (Yeats).
  A fish becomes a glimmering girl.

### Abjuration

- **Sanctuary / Shield / Protection** — Psalm 91 (KJV). "His truth shall be
  thy shield and buckler."
- **Bless / Guidance** — "Pied Beauty" (Gerard Manley Hopkins, 1844–1889). "Glory
  be to God for dappled things" — a spoken blessing.

## To finalize one

- Pick the poem and the spell.
- Decide the verse split — how many stanzas, how many readers.
- Decide what it foreshadows, and what the caster does and doesn't know.
- Save the poem verbatim into `handouts/` (line breaks preserved, fenced
  code block) with author, dates, and title, the way
  [*Wynken, Blynken, and Nod*](../handouts/wynken-blynken-and-nod.md) is kept.
