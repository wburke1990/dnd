# The Mughal Founding — the House of Mihran `[draft / new]`

The fuller writeup of the Mughal period flagged open in
[the fallen gnome kingdom](the-fallen-gnome-kingdom.md): the founder, his queen,
and his sons — the generation that crossed the mountains, took the gnome country,
and built the middle empire. The [Returner the party can meet on the Aar
road](../bestiary/copa-irish-beasts.md) is one of these sons, come home eight
hundred years after he rode out.

> **Real-world anchors.** The Mughal line follows the Irish *Book of Invasions* —
> the Mughals are the **Milesians**, the sons of Míl (as the gnomes are the Tuatha
> Dé and the Firbolg are the Fir Bolg). The founder is Míl and **Babur** at the
> same time; his sons are the sons of Míl — the poet **Amergin**, the warring
> **Éber and Érimón**, the drowned **Donn**. The queen's abduction follows the
> Hindu tale of **Krishna and Rukmini**, because it happens in
> [Kalikhat](nila-atlas.md), which is Hindu ground. Farhad's own story is
> **Oisín** in Tír na nÓg. Each deed keeps to the pantheon of the place it happens
> in; none are mixed.

## The crossing — about 900 years ago

The [Bad Pass, ~1,000 ya](world-history-timeline.md) — the sabotaged comet pass —
brought a volcanic winter over the world. It drowned the
[Muspel empire](the-muspel-empire.md) — its capital went under the sea — in the same
year, and its cold set off wars in the south. Those wars took generations to run. They
wore down the Mughals' southern homeland and, three or four generations later, pushed
them north over the
mountains — so the crossing falls at about **~900 ya**, not the round ~1,000 the
other records give it. They came into the gnome country of
[Suartleheim Eet](the-fallen-gnome-kingdom.md), three thousand years into its
decline, and took what was left.

## Sultan Mihran, called the Tiger

The founder. He led the people over the mountains and took the kingdom by the
sword, then held it until his death. The deed of his youth was the theft of his queen
out of the far north (below); the songs of the middle empire begin with it. He left
the kingdom to his sons, and they built it up and then broke it apart between them.

## The queen — Ratna of Kalikhat

Mihran's chief queen was a **drow** of [Kalikhat](nila-atlas.md), Kali's country in
the far north — the coast over the whole Underdark, where mortals first dealt with
demons. Her name was **Ratna**, and how Mihran took her is the story the middle
empire's songs open with.

She was promised against her will to a demon-lord of the deep. She had heard of
Mihran, and she sent him word: *come and take me.* By the custom of her people the
bride went up to the **temple of Kali** to worship the goddess on the eve of the
wedding — and as she came out from the shrine, Mihran rode in and carried her off
in the sight of everyone. The demon-lord and his allies gave chase and were beaten.
Her brother **Vetal** pursued swearing to kill him; Mihran took him alive, and
Ratna begged for his life. Mihran spared him and sent him home shamed. Vetal never
forgave it. He keeps the grudge still, below.

Ratna became Mihran's chief queen, and their son was **Farhad**.

*(Krishna and Rukmini, straight: the bride sends for her rescuer, is carried off
from the goddess's temple, the pursuers are beaten, the brother is spared at her
plea and never reconciles, and she rules as chief queen. Nothing is borrowed from
any other tale.)*

## The sons of Mihran

- **Siyavash, the eldest.** The first of the line to die — he drowned at a
  river-ford in the crossing, when a flood came down out of the mountains. He had
  offended a woman of the gnome country on the road, and she had told him he would
  hold none of the land he came to take. The Mughals say their dead gather at his
  house. *(Donn, and Tech Duinn, the House of the Dead — kept to itself; this is
  the Mughals' own house of the dead, not the [Amseh
  afterlife](egyptian-afterlife-mythology.md) next door.)*
- **Khusrau.** The poet and the first judge. He gave the realm its law and its
  songs; the Mughal courts of poetry and the Mughal law both trace to him.
  *(Amergin, who recites the land into being and gives its first judgment.)*
- **Bahram and Hormuz.** They split the kingdom between them, north and south, and
  then went to war over it. Hormuz killed Bahram and ruled alone as the first
  Sultan. *(Éber and Érimón — the founding fratricide.)*
- **Farhad.** Ratna's son (below).

## Farhad — Ratna's son, and the Returner

Farhad is Ratna's son, the one drow-blooded prince of the house — the rest of the
line held human. A **woman of the Land of Youth**, out over the western sea — not
one of the gnome fair folk of the mounds; the Mughals' songs call such a one a
**peri** — carried him off for what he counted as a short while, and he lost the
whole Mughal age. He is the **Returner** the party can meet on the Aar road: he asks
after his father and brothers, all centuries dead, and ages to dust the moment he sets
foot on the ground.

His scene, his lines, and the dying-questions the party can put to him are written
as a run-ready encounter in
[**The Returner and the Stone**](../bestiary/copa-irish-beasts.md).
