# Askr and Embla — the story the zealots supplied

> **DM reference.** The creation the Lonka Zealots teach at the two trees on the
> Jotunheim ring-wall, why it is false, and how the party can find that out. `[Will 8/16]`

## What the zealots teach

Two trees stand at the highest point of the ring-wall, an ash and an elm. The zealots
hold them to be the children of the primordial dragon, the first man and the first
woman, and they teach the creation at the trees to each new generation. Asked, the
trees give it back ([Lonka / Jotunheim](lonka-jotunheim.md) §5).

The trees are two dead of the forest-nameless of northwest Musleheim, taken as cuttings
on the march and rooted here ~1,900 years ago. The names are the zealots'.

---

## Why the story does not hold up

The party can find each of the following.

**It is attested twice, and the second source read the first.** In our world Ask and
Embla appear in *Völuspá* 17–18 and in Snorri's *Gylfaginning* 9, written around 1220,
by a man who had *Völuspá* in front of him. Two mentions, one of them derivative.

**They have no cult.** No place-names, no oaths sworn by them, no children named after
them, no shrines. Odin, Thor and Freyr appear in place-names, personal names and
poetry; the first man and the first woman are in two books. Nothing was built on the
story.

**The oldest text does not say they are trees.** *Völuspá* says three gods found two on
the land, *without fate* and *empty of might*, and gave them breath, sense, and colour.
The trees arrive with Snorri:

> *"When the sons of Borr were walking along the sea-strand, they found two trees, and
> took up the trees and shaped men of them: the first gave them spirit and life; the
> second, wit and feeling; the third, form, speech, hearing, and sight."*
> — Snorri Sturluson, *Gylfaginning* 9, tr. Arthur Gilchrist Brodeur (1916)

And *Embla* is not securely "elm." That reading is a reconstruction.

**It follows the Genesis account.** A pair made out of material lying on the ground,
inert, given breath by a god — *and the Lord God formed man of the dust of the ground,
and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life*. Snorri was a Christian writing two
centuries after the conversion, and his Prologue explains the gods as men out of Troy
who were misremembered.

**One manuscript has a stanza the other does not.** *Völuspá* stanza 65 — a mighty lord
who comes from above after the world ends, holding all power — **is not in the Codex
Regius.** It is in Hauksbók only.

`[for Doug]` The scholarly tradition for reading the whole of Norse myth as an import
is **Sophus Bugge**, *Studier over de nordiske Gude- og Heltesagns Oprindelse* (1881–89).
Overstated, and rejected in that form now, but he is the name for the argument.

## The pattern the zealots followed

Two documents from Doug's side of the shelf describe what was done at Jotunheim.

**Leave the shrine standing and replace its contents.** Gregory the Great to Abbot Mellitus,
601, preserved in Bede, *Ecclesiastical History* I.30:

> *"the temples of the idols in those nations ought not to be destroyed; but let the
> idols that are in them be destroyed; let holy water be made and sprinkled in the said
> temples, let altars be erected, and relics placed… that, when the people themselves
> see that these temples are not destroyed, they may put away error from their heart."*

A standing instruction to leave the shrine and swap the content. The zealots did not
cut the two trees down. They watered them, named them, and put their own story in.

**Retell the story in the local style.** The *Heliand* (c. 830) retells the gospel in
Old Saxon as a heroic poem — Christ a warlord, the disciples his sworn companions —
written for a people conquered and converted within living memory. The gospel told in
the conquered people's own poetic form. The zealots did the same thing: they tell the
forest-dead's origin as descent from the dragon.

## What the lie costs them

This comes out of the campaign's own cosmology rather than any argument about
manuscripts.

**Dragon-people are judged in the sky** ([lineages & afterlives](lineages-and-afterlives.md)
§4). The nameless go into the ground, unowned, and dissolve, which is the
forest-people's end (§5).

The zealots have spent 1,900 years teaching their children that they began as two
things rooted in the earth. They have taken the nameless' nature for their own origin
and built a place-bound faith on it — guard the ground and hold the island. Their older
creed was to keep moving ([Taniyn & Lonka](taniyn-and-lonka.md)). The story gives a
reason to stay.

And the dragons no longer come ([Lonka / Jotunheim](lonka-jotunheim.md) §5). The
zealots read that as an unexplained desolation. They tend two dead of the
forest-nameless and expect to be judged in the sky.

## How the party finds out

Six ways, in rough order of how cheap they are to run.

1. **The nomads have never heard of it.** The wandering dragonborn kept the older faith
   and have no first pair at all ([Blackacre](../characters/blackacre.md)). The trees
   appear only in the zealots' telling.
2. **Nothing else is named for them.** Ask the party to look: no ship, no child, no
   shrine, no oath, nowhere on the island or anywhere in the dragonborn diaspora. Do
   not point it out.
3. **Two copies of the book that disagree.** Give them the zealots' creation in two
   hands, and let one have a passage the other does not — the passage naming the trees.
   `[OPEN]` where the second copy comes from.
4. **The leaves are in a different hand and ink.** The pages carrying the creation do
   not match the book they sit in.
5. **The trees remember nothing earlier.** The nameless carry the memory they are given
   ([lineages & afterlives](lineages-and-afterlives.md) §5, §11). Ask the ash and the
   elm what happened before the gods found them and there is nothing there, because
   nothing was supplied. A real first pair would remember the early world.
6. **Pax carries the true memory.** The forest's common stock still holds what these two
   were ([the rite](../encounters/jotunheim-trees-rite.md), stage 2).
