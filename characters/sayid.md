# Sayid

Blackacre's former friend, the man he planned the ship-seizure with and then left. The
player-owned parts of this — the friendship, the revolt, what Blackacre did — are on
[Sam's sheet](blackacre.md) and stay his. What is here is how we run Sayid.

He first appears in play, without appearing, at
[the drifting wreck](../world/the-sea/encounters/the-drifting-wreck.md).

---

## What he holds

**A few ships**, not tribes and not a fleet. He came out of the revolt with them and
with the people aboard them, and he has been short of everything since — timber, water,
iron, cordage, hands. Ships that never make port take those things off somebody.

## What he wants

The floating fleet and the mainland brought back together. Sam gave him that goal and it
is close to Blackacre's own. Whether he means to do it alone or with the other races is
**open** — nothing written decides it, and Sam left it to us.

## How he behaves — the thing to get right

`[Will 8/18]` **He says what will happen if he is refused, and then he is refused, and
then he has to do it.** Not because he wants to. Because he said it in front of his own
people and he cannot be seen to say a thing and not do it.

That is what happened at the mutiny. He did not mean to kill that many. He made the
threat, the threat did not work, and once it did not work he had to follow through or
stand there in front of everyone as a man whose word is nothing. More people died than
he planned for. He has been managing what that looked like ever since — which is also
why Blackacre walking away hurt so much, and why he needs him back.

It happens again every time. He is a man who keeps putting himself in a position where
the only thing worse than doing it is not doing it.

**And he is clumsy at it.** He is not good at violence and he does not enjoy it. The
threats come out heavier than he means them, the work is botched, and it takes longer
and costs more than it should. Men who know nothing get taken apart before he gets to
the one who saw something. The ship he needed burns while his own people are searching
it. Whatever he sets out to get, he ends up with less of it than he would have had by
asking.

**How to play him at the table:**

- He talks first, and he offers terms, and the terms are usually reasonable.
- He states the consequence plainly and once.
- If he is refused, he does not argue, threaten twice, or work himself up. He gives the
  order and it goes further than he meant.
- He is watching his own crew while it happens more than he is watching the victim.
- Afterward he explains it — to his people, to a survivor, to anyone. He is not
  apologising. He is putting it on the people who refused him.

## What he is doing now

**Hunting Blackacre, hull by hull.** Every ship he takes gets the same question: where
have you last seen a dragonborn druid. He needs one — a fleet of wooden ships cannot be
kept up without a druid to grow and mend timber
([the floating fleet](../world/the-sea/lore/floating-fleet.md)), and the one he had was
Blackacre.

**The writ.** Two hundred in gold alive, half that dead
([the handout](../handouts/sayids-writ.md)). His people nail a copy to every ship they
take and carry the rest on to the ports.

**He wants him brought in alive** — to imprison, to bring him back around, or to have it
out with him first. Sam's own reading of the man, from the 8/17 chat.

## The cost of how he does it

At [the drifting wreck](../world/the-sea/encounters/the-drifting-wreck.md) he got two
masts. It cost him a hull he could have sailed, a hold of food, and fourteen sailors he
could have put to work. A ship that struck her colours would have handed him all of it.

Blackacre can work that out from what is on the deck. Nobody needs to say it at the
table.

## Open

- Alone or with the other races. Sam's question, left to us, still unanswered.
- Where he is when the party first meet him, and whether Blackacre goes looking.
- Whether he is right about anything, which is Sam's stated hope for him.
