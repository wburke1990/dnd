# Duha al-Warraq — character reference

**A Lonka Zealot at Jotunheim, and the one inside the caldera who argues for the
reconciliation.** `[draft — Will 8/16]` Dragonborn, devout, and has argued the same
position for most of her life; the caldera keeps rejecting it. *Warraq* is the old
dragonborn word for the one who keeps and copies the books; it is her office, not her
family.

> Companions: [Jotunheim as guests](../encounters/jotunheim-as-guests.md) (where the
> party meets her), [Blackacre — DM notes](blackacre-dm-notes.md) (the reconciliation
> she argues for), [Lonka / Jotunheim](../lore/lonka-jotunheim.md),

---

## Snapshot

- **Keeper of the books.** She holds the caldera's liturgical library, including the
  pontifical with the consecration of the two trees in it and the rite for undoing a
  false consecration three leaves further on
  ([the rite at the Jotunheim trees](../encounters/jotunheim-trees-rite.md)).
- **She is not a doubter.** She is more devout than the priests who overrule her, and
  everything she argues, she argues out of the creed. She cites doctrine for every
  position she takes.
- **She wants the dragons back** and would take Moku by force if the doctrine allowed
  it. She is not gentle and not a pacifist. She is a zealot, and better read than the
  others.

---

## What she argues

Two propositions, both orthodox, and together they contradict the fleet plan:

1. **Taniyn stopped and became the island.** She did not stand on Lonka and guard it.
   The dragon's own act was to arrive somewhere and end there
   ([Taniyn & Lonka](../lore/taniyn-and-lonka.md)).
2. **The holy ground is wherever the dragon is.** The dragons left Lonka and are
   gathering at Moku. So the holy ground has moved, and guarding Lonka after the
   dragons have gone is not what the creed asks.

Which comes out as: **we do not take Moku. We go to Moku.** Sail, arrive, settle, and
let the next generation go out after the dragons again — which is what every dragonborn
settlement in the world was founded by someone doing
([the Wandering](../lore/wandering-dragonborn.md)).

She does not know she is describing the nomads' creed. She has never met one. The scene
to play is Blackacre saying it back to her: she hears her own argument in the nomads'
words.

---

## Why she keeps losing

- **It sounds like cowardice.** *Leave the holy island* is what a faithless dragonborn
  would say, and the zealots define themselves by staying.
- **She has no standing.** She keeps the books. She does not lead anything.
- **The elders ask the rakshasas.** Every time the question is put, someone consults the
  demons, and the demons answer a little more than was asked, and the answer requires a
  blood rite ([Jotunheim as guests](../encounters/jotunheim-as-guests.md)). She has lost
  this argument perhaps a dozen times in her life and has no idea why she cannot win it.
  She reveres the rakshasas as much as anyone here.

**The demons do not know she opposes them, and she does not know they oppose her.** They
will never argue with her, never move against her, and never say her name. Every
consultation returns an answer against her.

---

## What she has noticed

She reads the old copies. The rites in them are shorter, and they take less blood. Each
addition is small and each has a date and a hand, and no single one is strange. Laid end
to end across 1,900 years, each revision asks for more blood than the one before.

She has not concluded anything from it. She has copied out a list.

`[for the table]` The list is the thing to put in front of the party. She has no
suspicions to offer them. Show them what she has written down and let them decide what
it means.

## How the party meets her

She is glad of a guest who can read. Jasper is the one she takes to the library
([Jotunheim as guests](../encounters/jotunheim-as-guests.md)), and Doug is the player
who will know what the book in front of him is. She shows the party things because
showing people the books is her office, and the zealots are proud of everything they
have.

She gives the party what they need without intending to.

---

## What she costs, and what it costs her

If the party proves the two trees are stolen, the caldera holds her responsible before
anyone else. She is the keeper of the account that turns out to be false, the one who
let the outsiders into the library, and the one who has been arguing for years that the
island should be left. Whatever they decide to do about the news, she is the first one
they will act against.
