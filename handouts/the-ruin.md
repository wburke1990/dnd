# The Ruin — the Work of Giants (Player Handout)

*A bard's lay / inscription over the giants' abandoned halls in [Suartleheim Weet](../world/nila/lore/nila-atlas.md) — the great empty stonework the dwarves live among, built by the [giants of antiquity](../world/suartleheim-eet/lore/the-southern-land.md) before they withdrew south. The poem names them outright: "the work of giants," broken and weathered. The full poem is longer; this is the opening.*

*Real-world anchor: the **Dorian Greeks among the Mycenaean ruins.** After the Bronze Age Collapse the Dark Age Greeks lived among Mycenaean "Cyclopean" walls they could no longer build and credited them to the **Cyclopes** — giants; "the work of giants" is their phrase as much as the Old English poet's (*enta geweorc*). In Weet it is literally true: real giants built and became that stone. This is the peaceful counterpart to [Suartleheim Eet](../world/suartleheim-eet/raand-copaa/lore/the-fallen-gnome-kingdom.md) — Weet **inherits** an older race that withdrew and died (the Dorians on empty ruins), where Eet **conquers** a living one (refugee gnomes taking the Firbolgs' land by holy war).*

*Real-world source: Old English "The Ruin" (Exeter Book, c. 8th–10th c.), opening lines, in Richard Fahey's translation ("The Ruins of Time"). The phrase "the work of giants" is the one Tolkien borrowed for the Ents. Excerpt; translations vary.*

---

```
Wondrous are these wall-stones, broken by fortune,
the citadels crumbled, the work of giants ruined.
The roofs are collapsed, the towers tumbled,
the pillars bereft. Ice on the arch
scarred the storm-wall, old, eroded,
weathered and worn.
```
