# I Hung on the Windy Tree — Hávamál

> **Handout — poem.** The Old Norse *Hávamál* ("Hovamol," the Sayings of Har),
> stanzas 138–141, trans. Henry Adams Bellows, *The Poetic Edda* (1923). Odin
> tells how he hung nine nights on a tree, wounded with a spear, sacrificed to himself, and
> took up the runes from below. Bellows prints a `|` in the long lines to mark
> the caesura; it is kept here.

## Stanzas 138–139 `[to verify — from memory]`

```
I ween that I hung | on the windy tree,
Hung there for nights full nine;
With the spear I was wounded, | and offered I was
To Othin, myself to myself,
On the tree that none | may ever know
What root beneath it runs.

None made me happy | with loaf or horn,
And there below I looked;
I took up the runes, | shrieking I took them,
And forthwith back I fell.
```
