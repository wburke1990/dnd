# I Hung on the Windy Tree — Hávamál

> **Handout — poem.** The Old Norse *Hávamál* ("Hovamol," the Sayings of Har),
> trans. Henry Adams Bellows, *The Poetic Edda* (1923), Project Gutenberg ebook
> 73533. Odin tells how he hung nine nights on a tree, wounded with a spear,
> sacrificed to himself, and took up the runes from below. Bellows prints a `|`
> in the long lines to mark the caesura; it is kept here.
>
> **Bellows numbers these stanzas 139–142.** Other editions number the hanging
> stanza 138, which is where this file's old 138–141 came from.
>
> Used at [the hell-tree](../world/suartleheim-eet/brauron/encounters/the-hell-tree.md):
> a god hung on a tree with a spear in him.

## Stanzas 139–142

```
139. I ween that I hung   |   on the windy tree,
      Hung there for nights full nine;
    With the spear I was wounded,   |   and offered I was
      To Othin, myself to myself,
    On the tree that none   |   may ever know
      What root beneath it runs.

140. None made me happy   |   with loaf or horn,
      And there below I looked;
    I took up the runes,   |   shrieking I took them,
      And forthwith back I fell.

141. Nine mighty songs   |   I got from the son
      Of Bolthorn, Bestla’s father;
    And a drink I got   |   of the goodly mead
      Poured out from Othrörir.

142. Then began I to thrive,   |   and wisdom to get,
      I grew and well I was;
    Each word led me on   |   to another word,
      Each deed to another deed.
```
