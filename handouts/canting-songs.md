# Canting Songs — the greeting and the job

> Two genuine thieves' canting songs, both public domain, used word for word at
> [Aar](../world/suartleheim-eet/raand-copaa/lore/aar.md) when
> [Beartholomew](../characters/beartholomew.md) meets
> [Aniess](../characters/aniess.md)
> ([the godstone dagger](../world/suartleheim-eet/raand-copaa/encounters/the-godstone-dagger.md) §1).
> The first is traded line for line as a countersign. The second is how he asks for the
> job.
>
> **Do not modernise these.** They are quoted texts and the house style does not touch
> them (`CLAUDE.md`). Spelling varies between editions; the readings below are the ones
> commonly printed. Farmer's *Musa Pedestris* (1896) prints both.

---

## 1 · The greeting

Thomas Harman, *A Caveat or Warning for Common Cursitors* (1567).

```
Bing out, bien morts, and toure, and toure,
    bing out of the Rome-vile;
And toure the coue, that cloyde your duds,
    upon the chates to trine.
```

**What it says.** Go out, good women, and look, and look — go out of the city — and see
the man that stole your clothes hanged on the gallows.

| Cant | Plain |
|---|---|
| bing out | go out |
| bien morts | good women |
| toure | look |
| Rome-vile | the city (London in the original; **Maalm** at our table) |
| coue / cove | man, fellow |
| cloyed your duds | stole your clothes |
| chates | the gallows |
| trine | to hang |

## 2 · The job

Thomas Dekker, *Lanthorne and Candle-light* (1608) — from **"The Beggar's Curse,"** which
Dekker prints as the song in which the canting crew curse the officers who take them up.

```
The Ruffin cly the nab of the Harmanbeck,
If we mawnd Pannam, lap, or Ruff-peck,
Or poplars of yarum: he cuts, bing to the Ruffmans,
Or els he sweares by the light-mans,
To put our stamps in the Harmans.
```

```
If we niggle, or mill a bowsing Ken,
Or nip a boung that has but a win,
Or dup the giger of a Gentry cofe's ken,
To the quier cuffing we bing.
```

**What it says.** The devil take the constable's head — beg bread, drink, bacon or milk
pottage off him and he tells us to clear off into the bushes, or swears he will put our
legs in the stocks by daylight. And if we couple, or rob an alehouse, or cut a purse with
a penny in it, **or open the door of a gentleman's house** — we go before the justice.

| Cant | Plain |
|---|---|
| the Ruffin | the devil |
| cly | seize, take |
| nab | head |
| harman-beck | the constable |
| mawnd | beg |
| pannam | bread |
| lap | buttermilk, whey |
| ruff-peck | bacon |
| poplars of yarum | milk pottage |
| cuts | says, speaks |
| bing to the ruffmans | go into the bushes |
| lightmans | daylight |
| stamps | legs |
| harmans | the stocks |
| niggle | to couple |
| mill a bowsing ken | rob an alehouse |
| nip a boung | cut a purse |
| win | a penny |
| dup the giger | open the door |
| gentry cofe's ken | a gentleman's house |
| quier cuffing | the justice of the peace |
| bing | go |

## How it runs at the table

**The countersign.** Beartholomew opens; Aniess answers. Two lines each.

| | Line |
|---|---|
| **Bear** | *"Bing out, bien morts, and toure, and toure,"* |
| **Aniess** | *"bing out of the Rome-vile;"* |
| **Bear** | *"And toure the coue, that cloyde your duds,"* |
| **Aniess** | *"upon the chates to trine."* |

**The ask.** Beartholomew says the four lines of the second song straight through. The
third line is the job: **open the door of a gentleman's house.** Aniess answers with the
curse's own opening.

| | Line |
|---|---|
| **Bear** | *"If we niggle, or mill a bowsing ken, / Or nip a boung that has but a win, / Or dup the giger of a Gentry cofe's ken, / To the quier cuffing we bing."* |
| **Aniess** | *"The Ruffin cly the nab of the Harmanbeck."* |

Then Beartholomew says what he wants in plain speech, and the scene continues.

## For Greg, in advance

Aniess's three lines and what they mean:

1. *"bing out of the Rome-vile;"* — **go out of the city**
2. *"upon the chates to trine."* — **on the gallows, to hang**
3. *"The Ruffin cly the nab of the Harmanbeck."* — **the devil take the constable's head**

(Lines 1 and 2 are the second and fourth lines of a four-line rhyme Bear starts. Line 3 is
her answer to the job.)

**Who can speak it.** Thieves' Cant is a 1st-level Rogue feature, so Aniess has it. See
[Beartholomew](../characters/beartholomew.md) for how he has it.
