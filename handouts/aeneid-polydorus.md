# Polydorus — the Aeneid, Book III

> **Handout — epic.** Virgil, *Aeneid* III.19–68. Aeneas lands in Thrace and
> pulls up myrtle shoots to dress an altar. Black blood drips from the broken
> roots. On the third pull a voice comes out of the mound: Polydorus, the boy
> Priam sent away with the gold, murdered by his host and left where he fell.
> The spears they killed him with took root and grew into the thicket Aeneas is
> pulling at.

## Dryden, 1697 `[to transcribe]`

One couplet is quoted widely enough to stand in as a placeholder. Read this one
at the table. `[to verify]` — the wording and the
surrounding lines have not been checked against the book.

```
Why dost thou thus my bury'd Body rend?
O spare the Corps of thy unhappy Friend!
```

Wanted: the blood dripping from the torn shoots, the third pull, and the whole
speech from the mound.

## Williams, 1910 — the plain gloss `[to transcribe]`

The same couplet, as commonly quoted. `[to verify]`.

```
Why wound me, great Aeneas, in my woe?
O, spare the dead, nor let thy holy hands
do sacrilege and sin!
```
