# A Poison Tree

> **Handout — poem.** William Blake (1757–1827), "A Poison Tree," from *Songs of
> Experience* (1794). A man keeps his anger, waters it, and it grows into a tree
> that bears one apple; his enemy steals the apple and is found dead under the
> tree in the morning. Kept verbatim, with Blake's own punctuation and spelling
> from the engraved plate. `[to verify]` against a plate facsimile (the Blake
> Archive) — the printed texts vary in their commas and in `water'd` / `watered`.
>
> Used at [the hell-tree](../world/suartleheim-eet/brauron/encounters/the-hell-tree.md):
> the party goes there to take one fruit off the tree.

```
I was angry with my friend:
I told my wrath, my wrath did end.
I was angry with my foe:
I told it not, my wrath did grow.

And I water'd it in fears,
Night & morning with my tears:
And I sunned it with smiles,
And with soft deceitful wiles.

And it grew both day and night.
Till it bore an apple bright.
And my foe beheld it shine,
And he knew that it was mine.

And into my garden stole,
When the night had veil'd the pole;
In the morning glad I see;
My foe outstretch'd beneath the tree.
```
