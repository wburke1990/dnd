# Rock-a-bye Baby

> **Handout — nursery rhyme.** English, first printed as "Hush-a-by baby" in
> *Mother Goose's Melody* (London, c. 1765). A baby is put to sleep in a tree;
> the branch breaks and the baby falls. Two texts below: the eighteenth-century
> printing and the common modern one. `[to verify]` the 1765 wording and
> lineation against a scan of the book — the early printings differ on
> "will fall" / "shall fall" and on where the lines break.
>
> Used at [the hell-tree](../world/suartleheim-eet/brauron/encounters/the-hell-tree.md):
> the fruits hang until they split, and a baby demon falls out.

## Mother Goose's Melody, c. 1765 `[to verify]`

```
Hush-a-by baby
On the tree top,
When the wind blows
The cradle will rock.
When the bough breaks,
The cradle will fall,
And down will fall baby,
Cradle and all.
```

## The modern text

```
Rock-a-bye baby, on the treetop,
When the wind blows, the cradle will rock,
When the bough breaks, the cradle will fall,
And down will come baby, cradle and all.
```
