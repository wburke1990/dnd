# Homer — the Making of a Sacrifice

> **Handout — the order of an altar rite.** Homer, *Iliad* Book 1 and *Odyssey* Book
> 3, translated by **A. T. Murray**, Loeb Classical Library (*Iliad* vol. I, 1924;
> *Odyssey* vol. I, 1919) — public domain. The steps of a Greek sacrifice in the order
> they are done: the hands washed, the barley taken up, the prayer, the barley thrown,
> a lock cut from the victim's head and burned, and the throats cut.

## The hands and the barley — *Iliad* 1.447–449

Chryses' hecatomb at Chryse. The hands are washed and the barley taken up before the
prayer.

```
and they made haste to set in array for the god the holy hecatomb around
the well-built altar; and then they washed their hands and took up the
barley grains.
```

## The barley thrown, and the knife — *Iliad* 1.458–459

The prayer and the sprinkling come immediately before the throats are cut.

```
Then, when they had prayed and had sprinkled the barley grains, they first
drew back the victims' heads and cut their throats and flayed them
```

## The lock of hair — *Odyssey* 3.445–446

Nestor at Pylos. The hair off the head goes into the fire before the victim is killed.

```
And Nestor, the old horseman, made the beginning with the washing of hands
and the sprinkling of barley grains, and earnestly he prayed to Athena,
cutting off as first offering the hair from the head of the victim and
casting it into the fire.
```
