# The Peseshkef — the godstone ritual blade

*Image-generation prompt, kept verbatim, with the lore it needs. The knife is the object
[Beartholomew](../../../../characters/beartholomew.md) asks the party to steal at Aar
([the godstone dagger](../../raand-copaa/encounters/the-godstone-dagger.md)); it is worn by
**Setha Renk**, who leads the
[Lapis Writ](../encounters/tavern-name-hunter-crews.md). Two prompts: the knife by itself,
and Setha wearing it. Setha's physical description below is the canon one — she had none
before this file.*

---

## Prompt 1 — the knife

I am making art for my D&D campaign. I need a single object study: an ancient ritual knife,
photographed or painted as a museum piece against a plain neutral ground, lit so every cut
mark reads.

The world, so you know what you are drawing. My campaign's ancient civilization is based
on New Kingdom Egypt — I call them the Amseh. Their empire collapsed about five thousand
years ago in drought and volcanic winter, and the modern city of Maalm was raised over its
ruins. This knife is Amseh, from that empire.

It is a **peseshkef**: the ritual blade used in the Opening of the Mouth, the funeral rite
where a priest touches the instrument to a dead man's face to give him back his senses —
sight, hearing, speech, taste — so his soul can function in the afterlife. In my world the
god who performs that rite is Anubis, jackal-headed. It is a priest's tool for opening a
dead mouth, and it is sharp.

The material matters. In my world the only metal better than bronze is meteoric — what the
Egyptians called *bia-n-pet*, "iron of the sky" — and this blade is made of a different
material again: **godstone**, the sentient stone that falls when the comet passes, the
stuff the gods themselves are made of. So the blade should not look like steel and should
not look like flint. It is a flat dead **grey with no shine at all** — no gleam, no
reflection, no highlight along the edge. Slightly granular, unpolished. It should look
dense: thick in section for its size.

The shape and the decoration:

- About a foot long overall.
- The blade **forks into two prongs with a notch cut between them** — the fishtail shape of
  a real peseshkef. The notch is the part that touches the mouth.
- **An eye is cut into each prong** — the Egyptian wedjat, the eye of Horus. One eye is
  whole. The other has the brow broken through, cut across.
- **The prong with the broken brow is worn smooth and rounded** from thousands of years of
  use. The other prong still has its edges.
- The **haft is carved as the foreleg of a bull** — the hoof at the butt end, the shoulder
  joint where it meets the blade. Same dead grey stone as the blade, one piece with it.
- Down one face of the haft, **seven shallow round cups** cut in a row, each about the
  width of a thumbprint.
- Down the other face, **seven small dots with a fine line joining them** — a constellation
  diagram, cut plainly.
- **Under the hoof, on the flat of the butt, a small mark: three straight strokes and a
  circle.**

The myth behind the decoration: in the rite the foreleg is cut from a living bull and
carried into the tomb, and the same shape is the *Meskhetiu*,
the seven stars of the bull's foreleg in the northern sky, which is where the Egyptian dead
go up — so the seven dots on the back of the haft are the same seven stars as the haft's own
shape. The seven cups are the seven sacred oils of the rite. The two eyes are Horus's, the
whole one and the one Set tore out.

Condition: five thousand years old and still in use. Not corroded — this stone does not
corrode — but worn where hands and lips have touched it, and grimy in the cut lines. No
gold, no gems, no inscription in hieroglyphs. Everything on it is cut into the stone itself.

If you can, give me one straight-on view of the whole knife and one close view of the haft
showing the seven cups and the seven stars.

## Prompt 2 — Setha Renk, wearing it

Same world, same knife. Now a portrait of the woman who owns it.

**Setha Renk** is a half-elf, mid-thirties by human reckoning, and she leads a chartered
tomb-clearing crew called the Lapis Writ. She is Maalm aristocracy on her mother's side —
founder-blood, one of the old desert houses, elves who have been rich in that city since it
was founded — and her father was not an elf: the ears a little short of elven, the bones a
little heavy for it. Her mother was the last of a thinned old house, and a rival house had
her killed. Setha is the last of that house.

Appearance: dark hair cut short and kept short, the way someone does who works in tombs.
Dark skin. Tall. Half a smile, eyebrows up, mouth closed. Good undyed linen, a long coat
the colour of sand with the wear at the cuffs, boots that have been resoled. Gold in her
ears and nothing on her hands. One piece of **lapis lazuli** at her throat or on a seal at her belt — deep
blue with gold flecks, the stone her crew is named for.

She wears **the knife described above at her belt, in the open**, on the left, hilt
forward, hanging from a plain leather loop. She has no idea what it is. To her it is the
piece her house has always had, and the only thing her family says about it is *"it cuts
better than it should."*

Setting: a raw new port town on a river mouth — timber wharves, ore dust on everything,
crowded, few finished buildings — so put her in the doorway of the one good building in it,
with a wine cup in her hand, in the late afternoon. Warm low light. Painterly, not photoreal.

## The pair

The knife's match is the **small Anubis** — a palm-sized jackal-headed statue in the same
dead grey godstone, ~5,000 years old, carried out of the fallen empire and now in
[Aniess](../../../../characters/aniess.md)'s hands
([the Dream Quest](../encounters/the-dream-quest.md)). **The same mark — three strokes and a
circle — is cut under its feet.** If a third image is wanted, it is the two of them side by
side on a plank, with the marks showing.
