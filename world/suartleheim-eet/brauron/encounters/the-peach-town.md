# PART ONE — BACKGROUND
*Read in prep.*

---

> Part of the Brauron quest — the town the party comes in through, at the edge of the
> poison deadland. See [the hell-tree](the-hell-tree.md) (the tree itself) and
> [the game preserve](the-game-preserve.md) (the hunt).

**Brauron** is the last town before the poison deadland, on the coast north of
[Raand](../../../nila/lore/nila-atlas.md). It is a town of **gnome artisans and
performers**, where the hunters and pilgrims gather and where the **priestesses of
Artemis** keep their temple. It was founded four thousand years ago by
[Momotarō](../../../../handouts/momotaro.md), a demon who emerged from a fruit of
[the hell-tree](the-hell-tree.md). The fruit floated down the river out of the deadland to
an old gnome couple, who gave him water and raised him
([the founding](../lore/the-founding-of-brauron.md)). A peach grove rings the town, his
statue stands in the square, and the gnomes perform kabuki plays about children who
emerge from fruit.

`[name]` Brauron is named for the real Artemis sanctuary where girls served the goddess
as bears. The name is Greek. The Muspel empire gave the map its Norse names (Raand, Aar,
Suartleheim), and this town is older than the empire.

---

## The people

The people are gnome artisans and performers, as in
[Raand](../../../nila/lore/nila-atlas.md). They carve, build, and act. They have a founding myth about a child who emerged from a
fruit.

---

## The Megabyzoi and the Attis mystery

`[DM background]` The Megabyzoi are real: Artemis's priests at Ephesus were castrated,
in the manner of the **Galli**, the priests of Cybele and Attis, who castrated themselves. Attis
castrates himself under the pine and dies. The eunuch priests perform the Attis
mystery.

---

## The bee-priestesses and Sarric

`[Sarric]` Sarric carries his beehive everywhere
([sheet](../../../../characters/sarric.md)), and the temple here has **bee**-priestesses.
Jeremy decides what Sarric makes of it.

## Read aloud — the square

> *The town is ringed with peach trees, the branches loaded with fruit. In the square a
> stone statue of a boy stands on a plinth, with writing carved into the base. In front of
> it, players in painted masks and robes are performing a play for a small crowd — an old
> couple, a great peach, a child stepping out of it.*

The play and the statue are **[Momotarō](../../../../handouts/momotaro.md)**. An old gnome
couple find a peach in the river and a boy steps out of it. They raise him, and the town
is built around their house. The passage where the peach splits is cut into the base.

`[DM]` The base reads *"Wait a bit, old man!"* Read it out. A newborn demon says the same
line at [the hell-tree](the-hell-tree.md).

---

## Read aloud — the fountain

> *A fountain stands in the square. At the spout a stone peach is carved split open, with
> a boy climbing out of it, and the water runs out of the split. Words are cut around the
> rim. The townsfolk fill their jugs here.*

The carving is [Momotarō](../../../../handouts/momotaro.md) stepping out of the peach. The
words on the rim are from [the Three Citrons](../../../../handouts/the-three-citrons.md),
where a maiden bursts from a cut citron and vanishes if she is not given water at once:

> *"Give me to drink!"*

`[quotation]` Burton's translation, word for word. Leave it as written.

---

## Read aloud — the puppet show

> *In a doorway off the square a puppeteer is putting on a show for the children. A puppet plants
> a seed; a paper flower opens; a tiny puppet girl sits up inside it.*

**[Thumbelina](../../../../handouts/thumbelina.md):** a planted barleycorn grows into a flower
with a tiny girl inside.

---

## Read aloud — the girls

> *A line of town girls in yellow robes walks across the square to the temple, each with
> a bear-skin over her shoulders. They chant as they go.*

```
I bore the holy vessels
At seven, then
I pounded barley
At the age of ten,
And clad in yellow robes,
Soon after this,
I was Little Bear to
Brauronian Artemis;
Then neckletted with figs,
Grown tall and pretty,
I was a Basket-bearer,
```

`[quotation]` [Lysistrata](../../../../handouts/lysistrata-the-bear.md) 638–651, tr.
Lindsay. The town's girls serve the goddess as bears for a season before they marry. Girls
did this at the real Brauron.

---

## Read aloud — the temple of Artemis

> *The temple is dim and cool. The priestesses are veiled and still; among them
> stand a few men with smooth faces. On the walls, in paint, a young man is
> gored by a boar, figures stand over his body, and a tree grows where his blood falls.*

**Two priesthoods serve at the temple, modelled on the priesthoods at Ephesus.** The
**bee-priestesses (the Melissae, "the bees")** are Artemis's virgin priestesses, and they run
the hunt. The **eunuch priests (the Megabyzoi)** perform the
**[Attis mystery](../../../../handouts/the-birth-of-attis.md)**. Attis is a young god born
from an almond. The almond tree grew where a killed god's blood fell. Attis dies, and he
is mourned.

The priestesses also serve Artemis as **Hecate**, goddess of the moon and the dead. As
Hecate, she is the goddess of the deadland and the hell-tree.

---

## The hunt starts here — dressing the quarry as the stag

The priestesses run two sacred hunts in [the game preserve](the-game-preserve.md), one
for beasts and one for a man. The man-hunt is modelled on the myth of Actaeon. Both are
set up here, in the temple. The cult dresses its people in the skins of the goddess's beasts:
the girls are her **bears**, the quarry is her **stag** (antlers and a deer-hide), and
the hunters are her **hounds** (hound-pelts).

A PC who volunteers as the quarry signs up at the temple. When a priestess takes down a
name, she asks first **where to send the quarry's pay.** Most quarry die in the hunt. The
man-hunt is a sacrifice to Artemis. At Aulis a deer was put on the altar in Iphigenia's
place.

**The full rite is in [the dressing](dressing-the-stag.md)** — the sign-up, the water,
the barley, the hair cut and burned, the crown, the antlers, the roll of the hounds,
the two readings and the Longstrider at the door. Run that doc from here.
