# The Flame of Ankalia — the Ealdfyr and the Bone House

> **DM reference.** Kuru's other clergy: the mortal fire-wardens of the capital,
> the rules they keep, and how Kuru takes omens. Companion to
> [Kuru & the phoenix](kuru-and-the-phoenix.md), which covers the god, the
> godstone, and the warforged. Legend: `[settled]` agreed · `[loose]` floated,
> not locked · `[OPEN]` still to decide · 🔒 DM secret.
>
> Built on Zoroastrian practice, used as-is — the Atash Behram, the mouth-veil,
> the sandalwood, the five watches, the corpse law, the towers and the birds.

---

## 1. What the fire is

`[settled]` In the innermost room of the temple at **Ankalia** there is a fire in
a brass urn on a stone plinth, and it has not gone out in about fourteen hundred
years. Kuru calls it the **Ealdfyr** — the old fire. It was lit during the war of
independence and has been fed by hand, day and night, ever since.

`[loose]` 🔒 The Church teaches that the first coal came out of the phoenix's own
ash-bed at its last rising, carried down off the mountain by the man with the
stone hand. Nobody alive can check this.

`[settled]` It was **gathered from sixteen fires**, and the gathering took a year:

| # | Source |
|---|---|
| 1 | A house struck by lightning, still burning |
| 2 | A corpse-fire (see §4) |
| 3–16 | One from each of Kuru's trades: the dyer, the brewer, the baker, the potter, the brickmaker, the glassblower, the goldsmith, the coiner, the armourer, the smith, the tanner, the chandler, the limeburner, the charcoal-burner |

Each of the sixteen was carried to the temple and purified — a fire kindled from
a fire, that fire kindled from a fire, over and over. The count of purifications
is recorded as **1,128**. Only then were the sixteen fed into one urn.

`[settled]` There are lesser fires — a town fire in most sizeable towns, a hearth
fire in a household that can afford the wood. The Ealdfyr is the one that has
never been relit.

## 2. The rules

`[settled]` A fire-warden approaching the urn:

- **Wears the breath-cloth.** Two layers of white cotton tied over the nose and
  mouth, so that no breath and no spit reaches the fire. It is never taken off in
  the chamber. A warden who sneezes inside must leave, wash, and be replaced.
- **Never touches the fire, and never blows on it.** The wood goes in with long
  tongs. The ash comes out with a ladle. A warden who fans the fire with his
  breath has fouled it, and the fouling has to be cleaned by rite.
- **Feeds it dry hardwood, stripped of bark** — sandalwood where it can be got,
  which is imported and expensive, and which worshippers buy at the door and hand
  to a warden to carry in for them. Green wood, resinous wood, wet wood, and
  anything that has held food or drink are all barred.
- **Burns frankincense on it** at each feeding.
- **Feeds it five times a day**, at the changing of the watches: dawn, noon,
  mid-afternoon, sunset, and midnight. A **bell** is rung at each feeding — nine
  strokes, three times over.
- **Carries a bundle of twelve stripped twigs** in the left hand through the rite,
  and recites from memory. Nothing is read aloud in the chamber; nothing written
  is brought in.

`[settled]` **Who may go in.** Fire-wardens only, and only men born into
fire-warden families, and only after initiation. Lay Kuru may stand in the outer
room and see the doorway. Foreigners may not enter the temple at all. This is the
ordinary reason a party gets stopped at the door, and it is not negotiable by
argument — see §6.

`[settled]` **Washing.** A warden entering service washes for **nine nights**
under supervision, in a walled yard, in a fixed order, with water, with sand, and
with consecrated bull's urine. It is done again after any contact with a corpse.

## 3. Who keeps it, and the bar on godstone

`[settled]` **The fire-wardens are forbidden the stone.** No fire-warden may take
godstone into his body, at any rank, for any wound. They are the only order in Kuru
whose members age and die.

This follows from §4. Dead matter must not come near the fire. A warforged is
godstone containing the remains of the man he was
([Kuru & the phoenix §3](kuru-and-the-phoenix.md)), and the remains stay in him. So
by the fire-wardens' own law, **the high clergy of the Order of Sunne cannot enter
the room where the Ealdfyr burns.**

`[settled]` Neither side says the rule out loud.

- **The Order's position:** the stone is the god. Where the god is, there is no
  pollution. The question does not arise.
- **The fire-wardens' position:** no warforged goes in.
- **In practice:** warforged stand in the outer room with the laity at every rank,
  general included. Nobody has forced the question.

`[loose]` 🔒 The fire-wardens are therefore the only body in Kuru that can call the
warforged unclean without it being heresy, and the only clergy with no godstone in
them — so they never hear the phoenix at all. As the god's mind fails
([Kuru & the phoenix §4](kuru-and-the-phoenix.md)), the Order acts on instructions
the fire-wardens cannot hear.

## 4. The dead, the bier-men, and the birds

`[settled]` **A corpse may not touch fire, earth, or water.** Burning a body fouls
the fire; burying it fouls the ground; sinking it fouls the sea. Kuru does none of
these. Kuru puts its dead on a tower and lets the birds strip them.

`[settled]` **The fly.** Kuru holds that at the moment of death something enters
the body and stays in it, and that it arrives as a fly. It spreads by
contact. Everything the law does about corpses is an attempt to keep it in one
place.

`[settled]` **The dog looks first.** Before a body is moved, a dog is led up to it
and made to look at its face. The dogs kept for this have a pair of dark spots
above the eyes, so that they are counted as four-eyed. If the dog looks steadily,
the death is confirmed. If the dog will not look, the body is left where it is and
the dog is brought back at the next watch.

`[settled]` **The bier-men.** A separate class of men carry the dead. They go
always in pairs, never alone, holding a strip of cloth between them so that they
are one party and not two. They live apart, marry among themselves, eat off their
own plates, and may not enter a temple, its outer room, or its grounds. A bier-man
cannot leave the class, and his sons are bier-men.

`[settled]` **The tower.** Outside every Kuru town of any size stands a low round
tower with no roof, called the **Bone House** — a stone floor, open to the sky,
sloped to a pit in the middle, with three rings marked on it: an outer ring for
men, a middle for women, an inner for children. The bier-men lay the body out
naked in its ring and withdraw. The birds come.

What is left is bleached on the stone by the sun for a season, then swept into the
central pit, where charcoal and lime are layered over it. Everyone goes into the
same pit. Kuru has no graves and no names on stones
([overview §2](../../nila/lore/campaign-overview.md)).

### Reading the birds

`[settled]` **The birds are the oracle.** A fire-warden stands at the tower wall
through the first feeding and records it. Omens are read off what the birds do to
the body.

What is read, in order of weight:

| Sign | Reading |
|---|---|
| **Which eye goes first** | The right eye first is a good death and a clean answer. The left eye first is a bad death, and whatever business the dead had on hand is to be abandoned. |
| **How long before the first bird lands** | Counted in the warden's recited verses. Fast is favourable. Past a hundred verses the dead is refused, and the family is told. |
| **Which quarter the first bird comes from** | The sky over the tower is divided into sixteen parts, and each part is named. The answer is read off the part the bird crossed into. |
| **What kind of bird** | Vultures are the ordinary answer. A crow first is a quarrel in the family. A gull first is a death at sea coming. An eagle is a summons — the Order is told. |
| **Whether the birds fight over it** | Two claimants for the body means two claimants for something the dead left. |
| **A bird that lands and does not feed** | The worst sign the wardens have, and they will not say what it means to a layman. `[OPEN]` 🔒 what it actually means. |

`[settled]` **Kuru does not go to war without a tower-reading.** A campaign
requires a body — usually a condemned man hanged for the purpose the night before.
It is not written down. The Order has learned which towers give which answers and
ships the body to the right one.

`[loose]` 🔒 **The readings have started disagreeing.** Two towers gave opposite
answers to the same question on the same day. The Church's explanation is that the
fault is in the wardens (see §3).

## 5. Reading the fire itself

`[settled]` The Ealdfyr is also read, at every feeding, and the fire-warden on
duty writes down four things: the colour, the height, the sound, and whether the
smoke leaves by the vent or comes back down into the room.

`[loose]` 🔒 **What it has been doing for the last few years.** The party can see
these before anyone explains them:

- **Parts of it give off no heat.** A warden can hold a hand where he should not.
- It **will not take sandalwood some nights** — the wood goes in dry and sits
  there, and the flames stay off it.
- The **midnight feeding has twice had to be done three times** before it caught.
- The smoke comes **back down into the room** more often than it used to, and the
  wardens have begun leaving the chamber between watches, which they did not do
  before.

This is the same failure as the erratic blessings and the contradicted omens in
[Kuru & the phoenix §4](kuru-and-the-phoenix.md). The Church keeps a written daily
record of the fire, going back fourteen hundred years, in the temple at Ankalia.
`[OPEN]` whether it is a quest object.

## 6. At the table

**Getting in.** The innermost room is closed to the party and arguing will not
open it. The ways that work:

- **Aniess is Kuru-born and Order-raised** ([Aniess](../../../characters/aniess.md)),
  which gets the party into the outer room, the town, and the wardens' company. The
  stone rule does not bar her, and the wardens still treat her as unclean once they
  learn what the Order put in her.
- **Buy sandalwood at the door** and hand it over. Every visitor does this. It is
  the one transaction a foreigner is permitted, and it gives the party a reason to
  talk to a warden.
- **Bring a body.** A party that arrives carrying a dead friend has a claim on the
  bier-men, and the bier-men will talk to anyone, because almost nobody else talks
  to them.
- **Be there at a feeding.** The bell goes nine strokes three times. Whatever else
  is happening in Ankalia stops.

**Things that go wrong on their own.** A PC breathes on the fire. A PC hands wood
to a warden with the same hand that touched a corpse an hour ago and does not
mention it. A PC walks into the tower ring to recover something off a body. Each
of these is a serious crime in Kuru, and nothing in the moment marks it as one.

**A warforged in the party.** Shepard is dead flesh in godstone
([Shepard](../../../characters/shepard.md)) and every rule in §3 and §4 applies to
him. A fire-warden who works out that Shepard is warforged will not be reasonable
about it.

**An omen the DM wants to deliver.** Deliver it as a tower reading. The party
attends a reading for a stranger, and the warden reads left eye first, and names
the quarter the bird came from. He says nothing else. `[loose]` 🔒 One tower reading made over
any corpse right now gives the same answer no matter the question, and the wardens
have stopped asking it: the bird comes in from the quarter of the sky the comet is
standing in. Nobody has connected the two.
