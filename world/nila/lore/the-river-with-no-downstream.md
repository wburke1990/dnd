# The River With No Downstream

> A wonder taken from a poem, per [myth & poem as literal truth](myth-and-poem-as-literal-truth.md).
> The poem — [There's No Earthly Way of Knowing](../../../handouts/theres-no-earthly-way-of-knowing.md),
> Roald Dahl, from *Willy Wonka* — read straight: *there is no knowing which way the river is flowing.*

## What it is

A river with no direction. No source, no mouth. The current cannot be read: a
float dropped on it does not hold a course, and two people watching the same
stretch will not agree which way it runs. It keeps no bearing — a compass turns
without settling, and a straight pull on the oars does not arrive where a
straight line would reach. It is not dark. Light works on it. From anything on
the water, you cannot tell which way you are going.

It is on no map and cannot be put on one. It does not run between two places.

## Where it goes

It reaches only what has lost its place. Not a standing city, not a marked shore
— those have a place already. A city that sank, a door no one can find: this
water reaches things that are no longer anywhere.

## The cost

You do not choose where you land. You row, the river brings you up against
something, and you do not know what until it is under the hull. It may be what
you came for. It may be open water again. You find out when you arrive and not
before.

It takes your bearings, too. A person who has rowed it cannot afterward say where
things are. They cannot retrace the way they came, point the direction of home,
or set the route down on a page. How much of that returns, and whether it returns
at all, `[OPEN]`.

## Guides

A guide here is someone who does not know the way, because there is no way to
know. That is the only guide the river has. Someone who has rowed it many times
can say less about where things lie than a stranger can.
