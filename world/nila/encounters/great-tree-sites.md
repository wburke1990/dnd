## Dodona — the oracle oak `[stub]`

The oldest Greek oracle, in Epirus, sacred to Zeus. It answered: priests read the
wind in the oak's leaves, the cooing of doves in its branches, and a ring of
bronze cauldrons that rang one into the next when struck. Its priests, the Selloi,
slept on the ground and never washed their feet. Petitioners scratched yes/no
questions onto lead strips (thousands have been dug up). The Argo carried a beam
cut from the oak that spoke and prophesied.

**Site idea:** a tree that answers — the location-stone mechanic made into the
tree itself. Puzzle: read the wind, the doves, or the struck bronze; drifts of
other people's lead question-slips underfoot. Sources: Herodotus (calls it the
oldest oracle); Homer, *Iliad* 16 (the Selloi). Register: Greek, region unplaced.

## The Golden Bough / King of the Wood — Nemi `[stub]`

At Diana's grove by Lake Nemi, the priest — the Rex Nemorensis, a runaway slave —
held his office by having killed the priest before him, and kept it only until a
challenger broke a particular bough from the sacred tree and killed him in turn.
He patrolled the grove sword in hand, sleepless, waiting. Frazer built all of *The
Golden Bough* on him. The same bough is what Aeneas breaks to walk into the
underworld and back.

**Site idea:** a guardian who must be killed to take his place; a bough you break
to challenge; the office is a death sentence you inherit. Fits a hunting site or a
duel. Sources: Frazer, *The Golden Bough*; Virgil, *Aeneid* VI; Ovid. Register:
Roman/Italic, region unplaced.

## Yggdrasil / Mímir — placed, reserved `[not for reuse]`

The Norse world-ash; Mímir's well of wisdom beneath it (Odin traded an eye to
drink), the eagle at the crown, the serpent Níðhöggr gnawing the roots, the
squirrel and the stags. Already in the world: Mímir's well and the Lonka
ash-and-elm (Ask & Embla). **Reserved for the Norse belt** (Musleheim / Lonka /
Jotunheim) — do not reuse elsewhere. See
[temple sites](temple-sites.md) and [Lonka / Jotunheim](../../lonka/lore/lonka-jotunheim.md).

## The hell-tree — the dead god at Suartleheim's edge `[building]`

A comet-god the True Giants killed before it could rise; a tree grew from its
body, its mind trapped in the wood, its roots reaching the deep. The fruit births
demons, and Preem sends crews to take the newborns' names before they learn to
guard them. Draws on [Anchar](../../../handouts/anchar.md), the Qur'an's
[Zaqqum](../../../handouts/the-tree-of-zaqqum.md), Kronos/Uranus with the Gigantomachy,
the Juniper Tree, and the born-from-fruit tales. See
[the hell-tree](../../suartleheim-eet/brauron/encounters/the-hell-tree.md); its setting is
[the game preserve](../../suartleheim-eet/brauron/encounters/the-game-preserve.md).

## The warforged becoming a grove — plot thread, on hold `[waiting on Greg]`

Two Druid-magic warforged replacing their rotting parts with living wood — two men
becoming a grove. A chosen-transformation thread, the redemptive counterweight to
the transformations done *to* people (the Order, the bound demons). On hold until
Greg is back (it runs through Aniess, the Order, and Shepard). See
[Kuru & the phoenix](../../kuru/lore/kuru-and-the-phoenix.md).

**They state the rule, and ask to be paid for it.** `[Will 8/15]` This is the first
place blood consecration is said out loud, and it comes from something that chose to
become a tree. Verbatim, to be said as written:

> *"Trees live a long time, perhaps forever if you consecrate them with blood. Bring
> me a sip and I will tell you more, I am thirsty."*

The party is told the rule and immediately asked for blood in exchange for the rest of
it. Same shape as [Mímir's well](temple-sites.md), which names its price before it
speaks. See [encounters before the Jotunheim rite](../../lonka/encounters/before-jotunheim.md) and
[the rite at the Jotunheim trees](../../lonka/encounters/jotunheim-trees-rite.md).
