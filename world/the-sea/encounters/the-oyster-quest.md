# The Oyster Quest

> **[idea]** This file holds the sources we might use.

An underwater quest. The party are taken in as guests, and the hosts eat their
guests. Possible setting: the hall of a merfolk king.

## Texts already chosen

- **The White Queen's fish riddle**, from Lewis Carroll's *Through the
  Looking-Glass*. The answer is an oyster. It goes on a door. Handout to write.
- **"The Walrus and the Carpenter"**, from the same book. Two hosts take the young
  oysters for a walk on the beach and eat all of them. It is placed past the
  door. Handout to write.

## Myths and tales of hosts who eat or harm their guests

**From the sea:**

- **The Sirens** (*Odyssey* 12). Their singing draws sailors ashore to a meadow
  full of bones.
- **The Laestrygonians** (*Odyssey* 10). Odysseus's scouts meet the king's daughter
  at a spring, and she sends them to her father's house. The giants eat them and
  sink the fleet in the harbour with boulders.
- **The Lotus-eaters** (*Odyssey* 9). Their food makes the guests forget home.

**Greek:**

- **Polyphemus** (*Odyssey* 9). He shuts his guests in his cave and eats them. Odysseus
  gives his name as "Nobody," and when the blinded giant calls for help, the other
  Cyclopes hear that Nobody is hurting him.
- **Circe** (*Odyssey* 10). She feeds her guests and turns them into pigs.
- **Lycaon.** He serves Zeus a cooked child and is turned into a wolf.
- **Tantalus.** He cooks his son Pelops and serves him to the gods.
- **Atreus.** He feeds his brother Thyestes his own sons.
- **Persephone.** She eats pomegranate seeds in the underworld, and so must stay
  there part of every year.
- **Procrustes.** He makes each guest fit his iron bed by stretching them or cutting
  off their legs.

**Folk tales:**

- **Hansel and Gretel.** A house made of bread and sugar, and a witch with an oven.
- **Baba Yaga.** A hut on hen's legs, behind a fence of bones.
- **Jack and the Beanstalk.** The giant smells the boy: "Fee-fi-fo-fum."
- **Kurozuka** (Japanese). An old woman gives a traveller a bed for the night and
  tells him not to look in one room. The room is full of bones.
- **"Goblin Market."** Already a [handout](../../../handouts/goblin-market.md).

## Order of the clues

1. Food the party are pressed to eat.
2. A host who gives more than a guest can repay.
3. The oyster riddle on the door.
4. "The Walrus and the Carpenter."
5. Bones.
