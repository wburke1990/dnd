# The Drifting Wreck

An open-water encounter, built for [Blackacre](../../../characters/blackacre.md) out of
Sam's 8/17 chat. Sayid is not in it. A ship his people took is, with the mail still
aboard: nine copies of a dead-or-alive writ carrying Blackacre's birth name and his
sea-name.

Run it on any day of open water. It fits the **Maalm → Brauron** leg in place of a
[sea-table roll](../bestiary/sea-beasts.md) — see the
[cold-run packet](../../suartleheim-eet/brauron/encounters/cold-run-taverns-to-thinkers.md) §3.
About ninety minutes. Uses the shipwreck-and-merfolk maps.

---

## What is out there

The **Nasira**, a coastal trader out of **Aal**, running peaches and oil up from the
Brauron coast. She was boarded before dawn. Her people are dead, her iron and cordage
and water are gone, and she is burning when the party raise her.

- **No masts.** Both went over the side and are still held alongside by the shrouds,
  dragging in the water. She has no way to steer and no way to stop.
- **She is listing to port and down by the stern**, following the current at about the
  speed a man walks.
- **The deck has burned through** in the waist. The fires left are in the amphorae oil
  and there is little left for them to burn.
- **The merfolk came up after the fire died down.** They have been at the dead since.
- **One man is alive** — Rustam, a broker in writs, riding as a passenger, in the
  flooded hold with a fallen beam across him.
- **The ship's mail is still aboard**, in an oilcloth packet in the stern.

**Who did it.** Sayid's people. The hulls that came alongside were lashed, not riveted
— fleet build, not dwarf-built ([ships and sea power](../lore/ships-and-sea-power.md)) —
and Blackacre knows the difference on sight.

---

## Why each of them is here  `[DM rulings — none of this touches the dragonborn lore]`

### Sayid — he is short of hands, and he lost his druid when Blackacre left

A fleet that never makes port has to take water, iron and cordage off somebody, and
that is most of what left this ship. **He also took three of the crew alive.** He came
away from the revolt with a few tribes and not enough
people to work them, and the one job on a wooden fleet that cannot be filled by a
willing volunteer is the druid's — the hulls need someone who can grow and mend timber
([the floating fleet](../lore/floating-fleet.md), *why a druid is gold*).

He had a druid until Blackacre left.

So he is pressing crews off traders and asking every one of them the same question,
and he is circulating paper on the man who left. When the writ says the work is still
his, Sayid means it as written: he needs a druid, and the druid he had is Blackacre.

**Two other things the raid does for him**, and neither needs to be said aloud at the
table: it feeds the tribes he kept, and it is visible. A chief who was walked out on in
front of everyone benefits from being seen taking ships.

### The merfolk — they are paid in the dead

`[DM ruling — open]` **Sayid's tribes deal with them.** A fleet on open water needs what
merfolk have — deep water springs, pilotage, where the fish are, a day's warning of
weather — and what the merfolk want back is metal and meat, neither of which the sea
gives them. So the arrangement is standing: when the drums stop, the merfolk come and
take the dead and the iron off whatever is left.

That is why they are out here, hours from anything, arriving at a ship nobody could have
told them about. They came because the drums stopped.

The party are not fighting scavengers over cargo. The merfolk are collecting what
Sayid's tribes owe them, and by their own understanding they are entitled to it.

**And it is why they want the man in the hold.** A live witness off a ship the merfolk
were paid to clean is a problem for the merfolk, not for Sayid. They will trade for
almost anything on the deck. They will not simply be bought off him.

**What Blackacre can get out of them**, if the party talk to them: which
way the two hulls went, how many were aboard them, and that there were three men taken
alive and still living when the raiders left.

### Rustam — he sells paper, and one of the papers names Blackacre

He dresses well because of his trade. **Rustam is a broker in writs** — bounties, letters of
marque, debts, notes of hand — working between Mukka and the ports of this coast, the
same trade that put a marque in Blackacre's hand from
[Preem](../../../characters/preem.md). He was travelling to Aal with a case of paper,
and Sayid's writ was in the mail he was carrying because carrying other people's paper
is what he does.

He has read all nine copies.

**What he wants:** to reach a port, and to come out of this ahead. He is not cruel and
he is not brave. He will thank them, he will pay for passage, and if he works out that
the dragonborn who pulled a beam off him is the name in his own case, he is looking at
two hundred gold.

- **He can be bought.** Silence for passage and a share, and he keeps it as long as it
  pays better than the writ does. He is straight about this if asked straight.
- **He can be left.** He is human, so Blackacre's rule about whose blood he will not
  spill does not cover him, and the merfolk are four rounds from the hold. Walking away
  from the beam is enough to kill him, and nobody has to swing at anything.
- **He is worth having.** A broker who owes them is a way to know what paper is
  circulating on them, in Aal and at Mukka both.

Do not steer any of it. Put him on the deck thanking them, and let the players work out
what he is.

## What the encounter settles, and what it does not

Blackacre learns he is wanted by name, alive for a price and dead for half of it, on
paper that is in every port. He does not learn where Sayid is, and does not meet him.

---

## 1 · The smoke

> Smoke stands off the water two miles ahead, black and low, bending south with the
> wind. There is no land under it.

A watch check against the weather DC picks it up early. On a failure they are inside a
half-mile before anyone names what they are looking at.

## 2 · The ship

> She has no masts. Both of them are over the side and still on their shrouds, dragging
> alongside in the water, and she is going with the current, stern first, at about
> walking pace. She is leaning to port. Where the deck was amidships there is a hole
> burned through to the hold, and the fire down in it is low and yellow and going out.
>
> There is no one at the rail.

**Coming alongside** is a check — DEX or STR, DC 12, against her drift and the masts
dragging. Failing it does not stop them; it costs a round and puts them on the
lee side, away from the burned hole.

## 3 · The shapes

Say this as they close, before anyone is aboard.

> Something comes up out of the water onto her side and goes over the rail. Then two
> more, further aft. They are dark and wet, and they come over the rail without
> standing up.

They keep coming for the first three rounds — **six merfolk** (CR 1/8) and **two
merrow** (CR 2). The merrow do the killing and are the ones who have been at the dead;
the merfolk carry the pieces to the water.

Anyone who watches instead of shouting sees what they are doing: they are taking the
bodies off the ship, not looting her.

## 4 · Aboard

> The deck is wet and slanted and part of it is not there. What is on it is in pieces:
> an arm in a sleeve, a foot still in its shoe, a head. The pieces do not add up to
> whole people.
>
> Aft of the hole: three crates of peaches, split open, the fruit rolling on the slant.
> Barrels lashed to the rail. Boxes piled and gone over. Amphorae in their frames,
> half of them broken, and the oil out of them is what is still burning.
>
> Against the port rail there is a cage the size of a big dog. Its bars are bent
> outward. It is wet, and it is empty.

| Aboard | What it is |
|---|---|
| The pieces | Fourteen of the crew. Nobody can tell how many until they are counted, and counting takes a scene |
| Peaches, three crates | Food and cargo. Worth 15 gp in Aal, more the further from Brauron |
| Amphorae, twenty in frames | Oil and wine; nine unbroken, 4 gp each. The broken ones are the fire |
| Barrels | Water — emptied and left. Salt beef in two of them |
| Boxes | Cloth, mostly ruined; a box of Aal ironwork the raiders missed |
| The cage | Empty, bars bent outward, wet. Whatever it held is the DM's, and it can stay unanswered |
| The masts alongside | Sound timber. Blackacre can cut a spare spar free and the sloop can carry it lashed along the rail |

**The fire.** It is going out on its own and nothing on the ship is worth burning. It
matters only where the oil is on the water — a swimmer under the port quarter comes up
into it. Anyone who puts it out has an easier time going below.

**She is sinking, slowly.** From the moment the first PC is aboard, count **ten
rounds**. At ten the stern goes under and the hold is done. At six the water in the
hold is over a standing man's head.

## 5 · The man in the hold

> There is knocking under the deck, aft, from below the water.

**Rustam**, forty, human, in fine clothing that is now soaked — a good coat, rings, and
a locked case of paper he will not leave without. He was below when they came aboard and
he stayed there. A beam came down across his legs when the deck burned through.

- He is **in an air pocket against the deckhead**, in the aft end of the hold, in water
  to his chin. At round six he is holding his breath.
- **Getting to him:** down the burned hole and aft through the cargo, swimming, DC 12;
  or the stern hatch, which is jammed, DC 14 STR to force.
- **The beam:** DC 15 STR (Athletics) to lift, or **two characters together at DC 12
  each** in the same round. Cutting the cordage fouling it first (one round, DC 10)
  drops the beam to DC 12 / DC 10. On a failure by 5 or more the cargo shifts and the
  air pocket halves.
- **The merfolk are working aft too**, below, through the flooded hold. If the party
  spend more than **four rounds** on deck before they go down, the merfolk reach him
  first, and they kill him and take him apart the way they have the rest of the crew.

**What he says**, once he can talk: they came out of the dark before first light, two
hulls, lashed and low, and the drums did not stop the whole time they were aboard. They
took the cordage, the iron, the water, and three of the crew alive. They killed the rest
on deck. Then they fired the oil and left. He heard no name.

He will pay for passage to any port — 200 gp when he reaches his correspondents — and
he knows what paper is moving on this coast, which is worth more than the money. What he
does with what he knows about Blackacre is above, in *Why each of them is here*.

## 6 · The merfolk

They are not here to fight over cargo, and the party can have every box on the ship.
They want the dead, and they are taking them. The man in the hold is dead as far as
they are concerned; he is in the water, and everything in the water is theirs.

**Talking works** — they speak enough trade to bargain, and a PC who opens with
something to give gets a hearing. They will hand over the whole deck for:

- The **box of iron**, which they want and cannot make.
- The **broken amphorae and the oil**, once the fire is out.
- The **bodies left where they are.** Carrying the pieces off for burial, or burning the
  ship with them aboard, ends the bargaining and starts the fight.

**Rustam is the one thing they will not trade for**, because he is what they were paid
to prevent. Three ways past that: give them enough that the loss is worth it and let
them say so, promise them he goes somewhere Sayid's tribes do not put in and be believed
(a real Persuasion, and they will hold the party to it), or fight them for him.

**If it comes to a fight,** the two merrow are the threat and the merfolk break when one
merrow goes down. At level 3 the party can lose this, on a slanting deck with a hole in
it. A PC knocked into the water is in the water with the merrow — one drags a prone swimmer
under, and getting back to the rail is DC 13 Athletics.

**Blood.** Anything bleeding in the water brings more of them up from below, two a
round, for as long as it goes on.

## 7 · The mail

In the stern, in a locker above the waterline, an **oilcloth packet of ship's mail**,
dry: letters for Aal, a bill of lading, and **nine copies of the same document**, each
addressed to a different harbour. Hand Sam the printed sheet:
**[Sayid's writ](../../../handouts/sayids-writ.md)**.

The *Nasira* was carrying it the way she carried anyone's mail, for the fee. Sayid's
people carry the same writ.

Also in the locker: forty-one gold in mixed coin, and the ship's chart of the coast with
her route marked on it.

## 8 · Off her

- **The spar.** The masts are alongside and sound. Blackacre can cut one free and take a
  spare spar off her, lashed along the sloop's rail. If a storm takes his mast later, he
  has it.
- **Salvage.** Cordage from the shrouds, sailcloth, the peaches, nine amphorae, salt
  beef. Enough to clear two hull boxes without a port.
- **The dead.** Fourteen of them, in pieces, and the merfolk want them. There is no
  burying them at sea except by putting them in the water, which is what the merfolk are
  already doing.
- **The ship.** She goes down at ten rounds, or drifts on burning if the party leave
  early. She does not reach anywhere.
