# Sea-Beasts — the water's wandering bestiary

The water is universal. Every people on Nila descends from one seafaring
ancestor (see [campaign-overview §5](../../nila/lore/campaign-overview.md)), so the sea
carries the monsters of **all** their mythologies at once — Greek, Norse,
Egyptian, Hindu, Chinese, Japanese, Slavic, Filipino, Zulu, Inuit, Polynesian,
Canaanite, Mesopotamian. Any ocean-going beast of world myth is fair to meet
out here. As with the [Egypt table](../../suartleheim-eet/maalm/bestiary/egypt.md), the myth is kept **unsanitized
and primary-source**, as everywhere in this world (see
[campaign-overview §3D](../../nila/lore/campaign-overview.md)).

**When to roll.** Roll once per **day of open-water travel**, wherever the boat
is on the map. The sea is the same water everywhere; nothing on this table
depends on being near a coast, a reef, a strait, or a named place, so the daily
roll stands anywhere. A leg blown off course or a run for shelter is a fair
moment for an extra roll (see *Getting lost* in the
[sailing system](../lore/sailing-and-storms.md)). Place a beast as a set-piece
when you'd rather choose than roll.

*Tags:* sea; wandering encounter region; ties to [sailing-and-storms](../lore/sailing-and-storms.md)

Rolls **1–6** are mundane sea life (mostly safe; some will stove a small boat).
Rolls **7–20** are mythological — most offer a **BOON** for the right dealing
and a **CURSE** for killing, eating, or robbing them, and several are a
**parley or a puzzle** with no fight in them at all, answered straight out of
their own myth.

## Running a sea encounter

The boat's safety is speed and getting away, not standing to fight (see
[ships-and-sea-power](../lore/ships-and-sea-power.md)). So a sea-beast is rarely
a stand-up battle. Resolve it in the same three beats as the
[Egypt table](../../suartleheim-eet/maalm/bestiary/egypt.md#running-an-encounter), fitted to the water.

**1 · Detection — who has the water?** Run it off the [sailing read](../lore/sailing-and-storms.md#a-voyage-is-a-run-of-watches):
the hand on watch's read is what stands between the party and being surprised by
something already under the hull. A won read = warning and a choice; a lost
read = it rises first.

**2 · Disposition — roll 2d6 reaction** (the [Egypt table](../../suartleheim-eet/maalm/bestiary/egypt.md#running-an-encounter)
gives the 2d6 mood table; it carries over). Most sea-beasts want a toll, a
meal, or to be left alone — not a fight. Food over the side, right of way, or
the right word shifts the mood a step.

**3 · Resolve — pass, parley, or run.** Three outcomes, in order of how often
they should happen:
- **Pass / parley.** Sirens, the Umibōzu, Sedna, the Kun — these are answered
  with the right word, the right offering, or the right silence, straight out of
  their myth. Always the first option (see [DMing principle §3](../../nila/lore/campaign-overview.md#4-dming-principles)).
- **Run.** When a beast is above the party's weight, the encounter is the
  [storm set-piece](../lore/sailing-and-storms.md#the-storm-set-piece) with a
  monster instead of weather: stations, escalating rounds, and the beast strikes
  **hull boxes** and puts crew **overboard** exactly as a storm does. Break
  contact — outrun it, or lose it in fog or a squall — and the boat lives.
- **Fight.** Only when cornered, or when the party has grown into it. Low-level,
  a real monster is a thing to flee, not kill.

**Scaling.** Like the [sailing system](../lore/sailing-and-storms.md#scaling-with-the-party):
at ~L3 the fare is the mundane rolls and the parley-beasts, with the monsters
run as hazards to escape. Mid-game, Blackacre's water magic makes the middle
rows fightable. High-game, the great primordials and the strait are the scenes
worth the name.

## Mythological Beasts (7–20)

### 7 · Sirens
*Monstrosity (Medium, bird-bodied women, 1d4+1)* — **Monstrosity, CR 4 each** · Neutral Evil
**AC** 13 · **HP** 52 · **STR** 10 **DEX** 16 **CON** 14 **INT** 14 **WIS** 16 **CHA** 20

Spd 20 ft, fly 40 ft. Talons +6 (2d6+3 slashing). **Song** (each creature that hears within 300 ft, DC 16 WIS save or charmed; repeat save each turn). A charmed helmsman holds the boat turning toward the song and will not correct — the boat comes about in a slow circle and the leg is lost until the song breaks; a charmed swimmer, or a PC not fixed to a station, is compelled over the side to swim toward it. Deaf creatures are immune. Bird-bodied, not fish-tailed — the older myth (Homer, *Odyssey* XII). They sing that they **know all that happens on the earth** and name the truth a listener most wants. **The answer is in the myth:** stop the crew's ears with wax and lash the one who would hear to the mast. A boat that passes with no one lost — the sirens' song broken — is said in the oldest tellings to be their death. **BOON:** a siren feather taken from one that dies of a clean passage lets its bearer, once, hear the true answer to a single question asked aloud at sea. **CURSE:** kill one in the water and its sisters' song follows the party across three more crossings.

*Tags:* sea-myth; d20:7; puzzle — wax & the mast; charms the crew off course

### 8 · Hippocampi & a Nereid
*Fey/Celestial (Large sea-horses drawing a shell, with a sea-nymph)* — **CR 3** · Chaotic Good
**AC** 14 · **HP** 45 · **STR** 17 **DEX** 15 **CON** 14 **INT** 12 **WIS** 15 **CHA** 18

Spd swim 60 ft. Hooves +5 (2d6+3 bludgeoning) — used only if the nymph is threatened. The Nereid speaks the tongues of the sea and asks nothing but courtesy and right of way. One of Poseidon's fifty daughters, ahead of fair weather. **BOON:** shown respect (yield the lane, a gift over the side), she gives a true course to a place the party names, or a day's calm following sea (treat the next leg as one step milder weather). **CURSE:** foul or rob her and she calls the swell up under the boat — the next storm at the hardest DC.

*Tags:* sea-myth; d20:8; benign; grants a fair course; do not offend

### 9 · Each-uisge
*Fey (Large, appears as a fine horse or a handsome stranger)* — **Fey, CR 5** · Chaotic Evil
**AC** 14 · **HP** 76 · **STR** 19 **DEX** 15 **CON** 16 **INT** 11 **WIS** 13 **CHA** 17

Spd 40 ft, swim 60 ft. Bite +7 (2d10+4 piercing). Change Shape (a beautiful horse, a comely rider, a lost swimmer begging aboard). **Adhesive Hide:** a creature that touches it while it wills (mounts the horse, takes the offered hand) is grappled, DC 16 to break, and the each-uisge bolts for deep water to drown and devour it — leaving only the liver to wash ashore. The sea cousin of the kelpie, deadlier than the freshwater kind, and at home in the open ocean; far from any shore it comes as a lone swimmer begging to be pulled aboard. **Tell:** its hair, or the stranger's, is always beaded with water and threaded with weed that never dries.

*Tags:* sea-myth; d20:9; shapeshifter; lures aboard, drowns; leaves the liver

### 10 · Umibōzu
*Monstrosity (Gargantuan, a black shadow-monk rising from calm sea)* — **Monstrosity, CR 8** · Neutral
**AC** 15 · **HP** 150 · **STR** 23 **DEX** 10 **CON** 20 **INT** 12 **WIS** 16 **CHA** 15

Spd swim 40 ft. Swamping Wave +10 (4d10 bludgeoning to the boat as **1d6 hull boxes**; crew DC 15 STR or overboard). Rises from a dead calm at night, huge, smooth, and black. **It asks a question** — *"Is the sea calm?"* or demands a **barrel or bucket**. Answer wrong, or hand it a whole barrel, and it fills the barrel — and the boat — with water until she founders. **The answer is in the myth:** give it a barrel with the **bottom knocked out**, or refuse to answer at all and hold silence until it sinks back down. A boat that gives it nothing and says nothing is passed over. **CURSE:** strike at it and the calm becomes the storm set-piece with the Umibōzu in it.

*Tags:* sea-myth; d20:10; puzzle — the bottomless barrel / say nothing; night calm

### 11 · Rusalka
*Undead (Medium, a drowned woman, pale and beautiful, hair always wet)* — **Undead, CR 4** · Chaotic Evil
**AC** 13 · **HP** 58 · **STR** 12 **DEX** 17 **CON** 14 **INT** 11 **WIS** 14 **CHA** 18

Spd 30 ft, swim 40 ft. Drowning Grasp +6 (2d8+3 + grapple DC 15; a grappled creature pulled underwater begins to drown). **Beckoning Laughter** (30 ft, DC 15 WIS or charmed toward the water's edge, moving to embrace her). The spirit of a woman drowned or wronged; she lures the living in to share her death. She rises beside a boat in the open sea as readily as anywhere. **Laying her to rest** — answering the wrong that drowned her — frees her, and the water she haunted becomes safe. **BOON:** a rusalka laid to rest gives calm passage through her waters ever after. **CURSE:** killed with force rather than freed, she rises again at the next river mouth, and the party's Bosun finds a hull box freshly sprung each morning.

*Tags:* sea-myth; d20:11; lure & drown; laying-to-rest option; open water

### 12 · Makara
*Monstrosity (Huge, crocodile forebody, elephant's trunk, fish's tail)* — **Monstrosity, CR 7** · Lawful Neutral
**AC** 17 · **HP** 115 · **STR** 21 **DEX** 12 **CON** 18 **INT** 10 **WIS** 15 **CHA** 14

Spd 20 ft, swim 50 ft. Bite +9 (3d10+5 piercing + grapple DC 17). Trunk +9 (2d8+5 bludgeoning, 15 ft reach, pulls a creature or boat toward it). The mount of Varuna, lord of the waters. It rises across the boat's course and **claims the water the party is crossing as its own**, letting no one pass who cannot answer for why they come. Reverence and a true purpose satisfy it; force does not. **BOON:** answered rightly, it grants passage and, once, escort through dangerous water (no beast roll that day). **CURSE:** killed rather than answered, the water turns against the party — every crossing one step worse weather until an offering is made to Varuna.

*Tags:* sea-myth; d20:12; gatekeeper; answer for your purpose; open water

### 13 · Bakunawa
*Monstrosity (Gargantuan sea-serpent, mouth a league wide)* — **Monstrosity, CR 11** · Chaotic Evil
**AC** 17 · **HP** 210 · **STR** 24 **DEX** 14 **CON** 22 **INT** 8 **WIS** 14 **CHA** 16

Spd swim 60 ft. Bite +12 (5d10+7 piercing; swallow on a hit vs. Large or smaller). **Moon-Swallowing** (rises at eclipse; while the moon is dark its presence drags an abnormal tide and the sea heaves — the storm set-piece runs with no downwind room). Tail Sweep (**1d8 hull boxes**, crew DC 16 or overboard). The serpent that rises to eat the moon; the old people beat drums and pots and raise every voice to frighten it back down. **The answer is in the myth:** noise. A boat that raises a wall of sound — drums, shouting, banging the hull (a group CHA/Performance check, DC 15, all hands) — drives it off without a blow struck. *Tags:* sea-myth; d20:13; eclipse; drive off with noise; drum-answer

### 14 · Inkanyamba
*Monstrosity (Huge, great eel-serpent with a horse's head, storm-wreathed)* — **Monstrosity, CR 9** · Neutral Evil
**AC** 16 · **HP** 138 · **STR** 20 **DEX** 18 **CON** 18 **INT** 10 **WIS** 15 **CHA** 16

Spd 20 ft, swim 60 ft, fly 40 ft (in its own storm). Bite +9 (3d8+5 piercing + 2d6 lightning). **Storm-Raiser:** its coming *is* the weather — when it hunts, the sky answers, and it moves inside a storm of its own making. A rising sea that no read can explain, and a serpent in the middle of it. Where the [saga mage-winds](../lore/fleet-warfare.md) are cast by people, this beast simply *is* one. **Killing it ends the storm.** **BOON:** its heart, eaten, lets the bearer read weather a day out (advantage on the sailing read) for a season. **CURSE:** wounded and left alive, it follows the party, and every crossing for a month opens with a storm.

*Tags:* sea-myth; d20:14; the storm is the beast; ties to the mage-winds

### 15 · Sedna's Tribute
*Celestial (the sea-mother, unseen; her children are the seals and whales)* — **encounter, not a stat block** · Lawful Neutral

No fight — a reckoning. The sea and everything in it answers to **Sedna**,
drowned by her own father, her finger-joints cut away as she clung to the boat
until they became the seals, the walrus, and the whales. She holds the
sea-beasts in her hands and gives or withholds them by her mood, and her hair,
unwashed, tangles with the grief of every wrong done above her water. The party
meets her as **empty water** — no fish, no seal, no wind, stores running down
(mark stores boxes each day) — and a hunger that does not lift until she is
answered. **The answer is in the myth:** someone must go down to her, **comb and
braid her tangled hair** (she has no fingers to do it herself), and set right,
or speak true to, a wrong the party carries. Do it, and the sea opens — fish,
fair wind, safe passage. Refuse, and the empty water holds. Her cold empty sea
can find the party on any crossing; it also resonates with the **northern
reveal** and the **sealskin folk** (see
[the-muspel-empire](../../musleheim/lore/the-muspel-empire.md), and Annibel's missing fleet
that sailed north — [ships-and-sea-power](../lore/ships-and-sea-power.md)).

*Tags:* sea-myth; d20:15; no fight — comb her hair, right a wrong; open water; sealskin-folk hook

### 16 · Hafgufa & the Lyngbakr
*Monstrosity (Gargantuan whale-island; the greatest of sea-beasts)* — **Monstrosity, CR 10** · Unaligned
**AC** 15 · **HP** 232 · **STR** 27 **DEX** 6 **CON** 24 **INT** 3 **WIS** 12 **CHA** 6

Spd swim 40 ft. The **Lyngbakr** ("heather-back") is the island that isn't: a low, kelp-grown back the party lands on to rest, water, or wait out weather (see *Getting lost*, [sailing](../lore/sailing-and-storms.md)). It lies still — until fires are lit or weight settles — then **sounds**, diving out from under everyone aboard it (all on the "island" DC 15 STR/Athletics or go into the water with the boat adrift). The **Hafgufa** is the greater beast that feeds by belching a chum that draws all fish into its open mouth — a whole calm patch of sea that is really a throat. **Tell:** an "island" absent from every chart, warm underfoot, with no stone and no true shore, that drifts against the current.

*Tags:* sea-myth; d20:16; the island that dives; don't light a fire on it; off every chart

### 17 · The Ketos

### 19 · The Kun
*Monstrosity (beyond Gargantuan — a fish thousands of li across)* — **wonder, not a fight** · Unaligned

Not an encounter to survive — one to witness. Somewhere in the open sea lies the
**Kun**, a fish so vast the party sails for a day along a flank they take for a
black coast. Then it **changes:** it heaves out of the sea and
becomes the **Peng**, a bird whose wings darken the sky from edge to edge, and

alive — stone into dragon into beast into island and back — shown once, early,
explained never. A party that has seen the Kun turn has seen the campaign's
secret before they know it is one. **[draft — a wonder to seed the
transformation theme; place it, don't roll it, and let it mean nothing yet.]**

*Tags:* sea-myth; d20:19; wonder not combat; seeds the comet/transformation cycle

## The Great Primordials — placed, not rolled

Beyond the table are three beasts too large for a wandering roll. They are
late-campaign set-pieces, and each ties to canon the campaign already carries.

**Leviathan / Lotan.** The uncatchable sea-dragon — the twisting seven-headed
serpent Baal and Anat slew in the Canaanite telling, the beast of Job's *"Can
you draw out Leviathan with a hook?"* An encounter that is not meant to be won
by killing: the sea itself in the shape of a dragon, to be fled, bargained with,
or survived. Hold for high levels. **[draft]**

**Tiamat — the salt sea.** The primordial salt water, mother of the first gods
and of every sea-monster, whose split body became the sky above and the sea
below (the Babylonian *Enuma Elish*). **This is the myth Tiamat, the ocean
herself — not the five-headed dragon-queen of ordinary D&D.** She rhymes with
Nila's own first mother, **Taniyn**, the primordial dragon who turned herself
into the dragonborn island (see [taniyn-and-lonka](../../lonka/lore/taniyn-and-lonka.md)) —
two tellings of the same act, the sea-beast that becomes the world. Use her as
the deep truth under the water, not a thing the party rolls initiative against.
**[draft — reconcile with Taniyn before use.]**

**Kāmohoaliʻi — the shark-god of the hot spot.** The elder brother of **Pele**,
who guided her canoe to new land; the shark-god who takes any fish's shape and
guides lost voyagers who know the chant, and whose waters no one may eat a shark
in. He belongs to the **Polynesian** water of Moku and Noku Kurunatuka — the
newest land, where the moving hot spot now sits and the finale falls (see
[campaign-overview §5](../../nila/lore/campaign-overview.md), [demons-and-the-hotspot](../../nila/lore/demons-and-the-hotspot.md)).
As the party sails toward the hot-spot islands, his sharks are the water's
warning and, to a crew that shows respect, its guide. **[draft — the sea-face of
the Pele/hot-spot cosmology; grows as the Kurunatuka arc does.]**

**Already in canon — cross-refs, not restatted here:**
- **The Dragon Turtle** holds the water at **Jotunheim** ([lonka-jotunheim](../../lonka/lore/lonka-jotunheim.md)).
- **The troll-women of the deep** walk the waves loosing a death from every
  finger — the orcs' sea-patrons at Hjörungavágr ([ships-and-sea-power](../lore/ships-and-sea-power.md)).
- **Taniyn** — the dragonborn creation leviathan ([taniyn-and-lonka](../../lonka/lore/taniyn-and-lonka.md)).
- **Apep** — the river-serpent of the Duat, kept for the [dream quest](../../suartleheim-eet/maalm/encounters/the-dream-quest.md), not the open sea.

## The strait — Scylla and Charybdis (placed by geography)

This one needs the geography, so it is **placed, never rolled**: one narrow
passage with a cliff on either hand, the only way between two waters. On one
cliff, **Scylla** — six long necks, each a head of three rows of teeth, twelve
groping feet — snatches sailors off a passing deck, **one per head that reaches**
(each head +13, DC 18; a caught PC is hauled up the cliff and eaten, and she
cannot be outfought from a boat, only outrun). On the other side, **Charybdis** —
a throat in the sea that swallows the water down three times a day and belches it
back, a whirlpool that takes a whole boat at the wrong hour (Navigator DC 18 to
time the slack; failure feeds the boat to the maw — **all hull boxes** and a
sinking clock). **The answer is in the myth:** you cannot have both. Pass close
under Scylla and **lose a few of the crew** rather than risk the whirlpool that
takes everyone — Odysseus was told to hug her cliff and grieve six men, not
fight. The strait is a choice about what you will pay. Run it as the
[storm set-piece](../lore/sailing-and-storms.md#the-storm-set-piece) with two
hazards and a stated price. The natural place on the map is the **strait dividing
Musleheim from Noku Kurunatuka** (see [nila-atlas](../../nila/lore/nila-atlas.md)).
